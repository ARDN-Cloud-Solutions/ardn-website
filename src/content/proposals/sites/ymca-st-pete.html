What this proposal says

The Y runs 24 distinct capabilities across about seventeen vendors. We went through every one of them against two codebases we already own, and the answer is not uniform — so this proposal does not pretend it is.

7systems retire outright
8retire in part
7the Y keeps, we connect
2the Y keeps, untouched

Why we can say this with confidence

Ardn has two membership products. The YMCA product has 26 feature modules. The Invited Clubs product — which was built on the YMCA codebase — has around a hundred. Everything Invited has can come back the way it went out.

So the question for each row was not “could we build this?” but “is it already built, and where?” Every claim below is tied to a file count and a line count in one of those two repositories. Where nothing exists in either, the row says so.

Two things on the 15 October sheet have moved. Salesforce was listed as needing a lead importer — Invited has three working Salesforce importers with 886 lines of tests behind them. Listen360 was listed as a port — Invited's survey engine is further along than that, with five shipped survey types and a 644-line test suite. Both are now straight retires.

The one thing that sets the date

Licensed childcare is the largest build in the programme, and it is the only reason the Daxko contract cannot close completely. We checked both codebases for staff-to-child ratios, licensing packets, classroom ratios and school-readiness reporting. There were zero matches in either product. That is net-new work and it is the critical path — everything else on the Day 1 list is a port, a connector, or a gap measured in weeks.

What we are not claiming

Four things are genuinely absent from both products and are named as such: licensed childcare, sports leagues, the Welld insurance feed, and migration tooling to get data off Daxko. A fifth — national sex-offender registry data — is a vendor feed to buy, not code to write. None of these are hidden in a footnote; they each have a row.

What has to be built

These are the gaps. They are listed first because they set the schedule, and because a proposal that buries them is not worth reading.

1. Licensed childcare and preschool DAY 1

Parent sign in and out, staff-to-child ratios, incident records, health records, and ELC reimbursement reporting. Nothing exists in either product — we checked for each of those concepts by name and found zero matches. This is the largest single build and the critical path to closing Daxko.

2. Migration off Daxko DAY 1

Members, families, barcodes, stored cards and history. No import tooling exists yet; it needs Daxko export access and an NDA before it can even be designed. Stored cards move processor-to-processor and never pass through us.

3. Online program and camp registration DAY 1

A parent cannot yet register a child online, early-bird pricing is not charged, and waivers are not enforced. Programs and Classes exist; these three behaviours do not.

4. Sports leagues

TeamSideline cannot integrate, and nothing in either product covers leagues. Buildable for what the Y actually uses — but run TeamSideline alongside for one season rather than racing it.

5. The Welld insurance feed

A daily member-usage file. Blocked on Welld publishing its format, not on engineering effort.

6. Sex-offender registry data

The screening workflow and its audit trail exist in Invited. The national registry data source does not, in either product. That is a feed to buy.

Three further items are small but real: the daily sales-and-joins tracker Craig keeps in Excel, corporate-partner discounts and promo codes, and PT package expiry, refunds and balances.

How this runs

Nothing is switched off before its replacement is live and the Y has signed off on it. The order below follows what blocks what, not what is easiest to show.

What moves first, because it has a date on it

DocuSign ends at the end of April. Invited's contracts and e-signature — templates, prefill, reminders, signed PDFs — is a port, not a build. It needs the state's own PDF field-filling added. This is the one item with an external deadline, so it goes first.

Then the ports, because they are already written

The member mobile app, surveys, Salesforce replacement, appointments and tours, and financial assistance are all working code in Invited. These come back across in the order the Y wants them, and each one ends a contract.

In parallel, the long build

Licensed childcare starts on day one and runs alongside everything else, because it is the critical path and it is the only thing standing between the Y and a closed Daxko contract.

Last, the things that need someone else first

The Welld feed waits on Welld's file format. Operate.Fit waits on Carla's requirements. Clarissa waits on the vendor's integration detail. Daxko migration waits on export access and an NDA. We will not promise dates on these until the dependency clears, and we will say which ones are waiting at every check-in.

Still open, and they are the Y's calls. Which 19 reports the Y actually needs. Whether the website moves off Accrisoft — Susie's decision. Whether Raiser's Edge stays connected or is replaced by Invited's fundraising suite once the Y has seen it. Whether SGA stays as the accounting system of record.
