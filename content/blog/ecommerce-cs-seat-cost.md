<p>General-purpose helpdesk platforms like Zendesk and Freshdesk price by agent seat — we've covered where that model strains. Ecommerce-focused helpdesk tools took a different approach: several price by monthly ticket volume instead, with unlimited agent seats included. That's a genuinely different cost mechanic, and it changes where the real risk sits for a seasonal ecommerce business.</p>

<h2>How ticket-volume pricing actually works</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Tiers by ticket count, not headcount.</strong> Each plan includes a monthly ticket allowance — a fixed number of resolved conversations — rather than a per-agent seat price, so adding staff to handle a surge doesn't add licensing cost the way it would on a seat-based platform.</li>
  <li><strong>Overage priced per ticket past the allowance.</strong> Once the monthly ticket count exceeds the plan's tier, additional tickets are billed individually — a lower per-ticket rate at higher tiers, but a real cost once volume spikes past what was budgeted.</li>
  <li><strong>Seasonal spikes hit the ticket meter directly.</strong> A BFCM or holiday surge in order-status and return questions runs straight into the ticket-count allowance regardless of how many temporary agents you staff up to answer them — the seasonal cost driver moves from "temporary seats" to "temporary ticket volume."</li>
  <li><strong>AI-handled conversations can be billed twice.</strong> Some platforms charge both the underlying ticket fee and a separate automation fee for AI-agent-resolved conversations, which is a less obvious cost layer worth checking for specifically if AI deflection is part of the support strategy.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does ticket-volume pricing mean unlimited agents at no extra cost?</h3>
<p>Typically yes for seats specifically — the constraint moves to ticket volume instead, so a business that scales staff but keeps ticket count controlled (via self-service deflection, for example) can genuinely benefit from this model.</p>

<h3>Which model is cheaper for a seasonal ecommerce business?</h3>
<p>It depends on which variable swings harder — a business that staffs up heavily but keeps ticket volume per agent low may do better on ticket-based pricing; one with stable headcount but unpredictable ticket spikes may find the reverse true. Model both against your actual seasonal pattern before committing.</p>

<h3>Does self-service deflection help under either pricing model?</h3>
<p>Yes — reducing ticket volume for repetitive questions lowers cost under ticket-based pricing directly, and under seat-based pricing it reduces the agent headcount needed to handle the same order volume.</p>
