<p>Chiropractic and physical therapy practice management software look similar from the outside — scheduling, documentation, billing — but the two categories tend to price differently in a way that matters for anyone budgeting a multi-provider clinic. Mainstream chiropractic platforms mostly charge a flat rate per licensed provider, reported in the roughly $50–$250 per provider per month range depending on the vendor and tier. Physical therapy tools more often break from that pattern entirely, billing by visit volume or as a percentage of collections — meaning the software cost scales with clinic revenue and patient throughput, not headcount.</p>

<h2>Two different pricing mechanics, one adjacent category</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Chiropractic: flat per-provider seat pricing.</strong> The dominant chiropractic-specific platforms bill a fixed monthly rate per licensed provider, similar to the standard per-seat SaaS model used across most practice management software.</li>
  <li><strong>Physical therapy: visit-volume tiers.</strong> Some PT-specific EMR platforms price in tiers based on monthly visit volume rather than provider count, so a multi-provider clinic running high patient throughput can land in a materially higher tier than its headcount alone would suggest.</li>
  <li><strong>Physical therapy: percentage-of-collections billing.</strong> Other PT platforms bundle revenue cycle management with the software subscription and charge a percentage of collections — commonly reported in the 4–6% range — instead of, or on top of, a flat subscription fee. That model ties the software bill directly to the clinic's revenue rather than its size.</li>
  <li><strong>Multi-location clinics compound either model.</strong> Whether billed per provider, per visit, or as a percentage of collections, a growing multi-location group scales its software cost right alongside its patient volume, with no ceiling built into either mechanic.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why the percentage-of-collections model deserves scrutiny</h2>
<p>A flat per-provider fee is at least predictable — it doesn't change month to month. A percentage-of-collections model does the opposite: the better the clinic performs financially, the more the software costs, with no cap tied to actual system usage. For a growing PT practice, that can mean the software bill is growing faster than headcount, referrals, or any other operational metric would justify.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Is percentage-of-collections pricing always worse than a flat fee?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily for a very small or new practice, where a percentage model avoids a large fixed cost before revenue exists. The case against it strengthens as the practice grows and collections increase — the software cost then rises without a corresponding rise in actual system usage.</p>

<h3>Does visit-volume pricing punish clinics for treating more patients?</h3>
<p>Structurally, yes — crossing a visit-volume tier threshold pushes the clinic into a higher rate, so a clinic's clinical success (more patients seen) directly increases its software cost, independent of provider headcount.</p>

<h3>Can a custom administrative layer coexist with our existing chiropractic or PT EMR?</h3>
<p>Yes — it's scoped to sync with the existing EMR via API for documentation and compliance-specific charting, while handling scheduling, intake, and patient communication on a flat fee outside the per-visit or percentage-of-collections billing.</p>
