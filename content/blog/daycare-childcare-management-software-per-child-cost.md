<p>Childcare center platforms — Brightwheel, Lillio (formerly HiMama), and Procare are the names that come up most — bill against enrolled child count rather than staff seats. That sounds like a minor distinction, but it changes the economics of the bill in a way per-seat software doesn't: a childcare center's enrollment moves constantly, on waitlists, withdrawals, and part-time/full-time swaps, and every one of those moves shows up on the next invoice.</p>

<h2>How the per-child model actually works</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Base fee plus per-child add-on.</strong> Reported figures put Brightwheel's structure at a base fee in the $15-30/month range plus roughly $2-3 per enrolled child per month on its Premium tier, though the vendor doesn't publish a public rate card and centers typically negotiate a quote.</li>
  <li><strong>Transparent flat per-child pricing.</strong> Lillio publishes a more straightforward per-child rate, reported around $8-12 per child per month — for a 25-child center that's roughly $2,400-3,600 a year just for the core platform, before add-ons.</li>
  <li><strong>Enrollment churn hits the bill immediately.</strong> Unlike a per-seat CRM where headcount changes maybe once a quarter, a childcare center's roster turns over weekly — waitlist admits, mid-year withdrawals, and part-time-to-full-time conversions. Each one is a billing event, not a budget-cycle decision.</li>
  <li><strong>Multi-site operators pay per location on top.</strong> Centers running more than one location typically license each site's roster separately, so consolidating parent-communication or billing workflows across sites doesn't reduce the per-child charge at any individual location.</li>
</ul>

<p>This is a genuinely different dynamic from the annual, enrollment-locked contracts school districts sign for K-12 student information systems (see <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/blog/school-district-sis-per-student-cost">student information system per-student costs</a>) — a district's enrollment resets once a year and the contract is priced against that snapshot, while a private childcare center's bill recalculates continuously against a roster that never sits still.</p>

<h2>Where the licensed platform still earns its keep</h2>
<p>Ratio compliance tracking, immunization records, and state licensing documentation carry real regulatory weight, and the check-in/check-out and ratio-alert workflows built into Brightwheel, Lillio, or Procare reflect years of childcare-specific compliance work. That core compliance record is the wrong place to look for savings — the cost problem is the per-child billing on top of it, not the compliance engine itself.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a custom portal replace our compliance and ratio-tracking system?</h3>
<p>No — it's built to sync with your existing childcare platform via API. Ratio alerts, immunization records, and licensing documentation stay in the compliance system; the portal covers the parent-facing and administrative workload that doesn't need that compliance data model.</p>

<h3>Why does our bill change every month even when staffing hasn't?</h3>
<p>Per-child pricing means the invoice recalculates against current enrollment, so waitlist admits, withdrawals, and part-time/full-time conversions all move the bill directly, independent of any staffing change.</p>

<h3>Is this different from how school districts are billed?</h3>
<p>Yes — a district's student information system is typically priced against an annual enrollment snapshot locked in for the contract term. A private childcare center's per-child bill recalculates continuously against a roster that changes weekly, which is a meaningfully different cost dynamic.</p>
