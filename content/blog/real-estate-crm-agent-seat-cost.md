<p>Brokerage-focused CRM platforms in the kvCORE/Follow Up Boss/BoomTown category price primarily per agent seat, which means a brokerage's core recruiting strategy — adding agents — directly raises its software bill every time it succeeds. That mechanic is rarely front of mind during a recruiting push, but it shows up clearly at the next renewal.</p>

<h2>Where the real estate CRM bill comes from</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Per-agent seat licenses.</strong> Every producing agent, and often every team lead or assistant with CRM access, needs an individual license — a cost that scales directly with headcount and agent turnover.</li>
  <li><strong>Lead routing and distribution add-ons.</strong> Automated lead-routing rules, round-robin assignment, and speed-to-lead automation are frequently priced as separate modules on top of the base seat fee.</li>
  <li><strong>IDX/website integration fees.</strong> Connecting the CRM to an IDX-compliant website feed for listing data often carries its own recurring fee, distinct from the core CRM subscription.</li>
  <li><strong>Inactive and part-time agent seats.</strong> Brokerages with a large roster of part-time or low-production agents often pay full seat price for people who log in rarely — the same "light user" problem seen in other per-seat CRMs, just with real estate-specific licensing names.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why this hits growing brokerages especially hard</h2>
<p>A brokerage's growth strategy is usually agent recruitment — the more agents on the roster, the more transaction volume the brokerage can support. Because most brokerage CRMs bill per agent seat, that exact growth strategy is also what drives software cost up, creating a margin drag that's easy to miss when recruiting success is being measured in agent count, not software spend.</p>

<h2>Where the fix actually lives</h2>
<p>An off-the-shelf brokerage CRM earns its fee when agents genuinely use its full feature set — IDX-driven websites, drip marketing, and transaction management all in one system. <h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Can part-time or low-production agents be moved to a cheaper tier?</h3>
<p>Some platforms offer a limited/view-only tier at reduced cost — check what's available in your specific contract, since not every vendor offers a meaningfully cheaper option for infrequent users.</p>

<h3>Does a custom agent portal replace lead routing entirely?</h3>
<p>It can be built to replicate your specific routing rules — round-robin, geographic zones, production tiers — without paying for a generic add-on module that may include routing logic your brokerage doesn't use.</p>

<h3>Who should review agent seat cost at a brokerage?</h3>
<p>Whoever manages the CRM vendor relationship, ideally aligned with the recruiting calendar — a seat review right before a major recruiting push, and again right after, catches both onboarding and inactive-seat cleanup at the moments they matter most.</p>
