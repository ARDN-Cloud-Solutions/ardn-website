<p>Multi-chapter organizations — national associations with local affiliates, franchised nonprofits, alumni networks — often use chapter management software priced per member rather than per staff seat. It's a different billing mechanism from the per-seat CRM problem most cost-reduction advice targets, and it has a specific failure mode: the software bill grows in direct proportion to your organizational success at growing membership.</p>

<h2>Why per-member pricing is a different problem than per-seat pricing</h2>
<p>Per-seat software charges for the number of people using the tool. Per-member chapter software charges for the number of people the tool is about — whether or not those members ever log into anything. A national organization with 50 staff and 40,000 members isn't paying for 50 seats; it's paying for a rate multiplied by 40,000, regardless of headcount. Growing your membership — the actual goal of the organization — directly grows the software bill.</p>

<h2>Where this shows up hardest</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Rapid chapter growth.</strong> A new chapter launch or a successful membership drive can jump your per-member cost significantly in a single renewal cycle.</li>
  <li><strong>Multi-tier structures.</strong> National body, regional bodies, and local chapters each managing overlapping member data can mean paying per-member fees more than once for the same person across different system tiers.</li>
  <li><strong>Engagement-independent billing.</strong> A member who renews once a year and never logs in costs the same as one who's highly engaged — the fee is based on membership status, not platform usage.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Can we renegotiate per-member pricing directly with our vendor?</h3>
<p>Often, yes, particularly at volume tiers or renewal — it's worth attempting before anything else. It typically lowers the rate rather than removing the per-member mechanism entirely, so the underlying growth curve stays.</p>

<h3>Does a chapter portal replace our national membership database?</h3>
<p>No. It's built to sync with your existing national or AMS system through its API — the portal handles chapter-level operations while your core system remains the source of truth for membership records.</p>

<h3>Does this apply to franchised or affiliate models outside traditional associations?</h3>
<p>Yes — any structure with a national or central body plus local units billed per-member (alumni networks, franchise associations, affiliate nonprofits) runs into the same mechanism and the same options for addressing it.</p>
