<p>Funeral home management platforms — Halcyon and Passare are the names most commonly cited — typically start with a base subscription fee and layer a per-case charge on top for larger operators. Reported figures put Halcyon's base around $295/month, with an additional case fee kicking in once volume crosses a threshold. That structure is similar in shape to visit- or volume-based billing seen in a few other verticals, but the underlying volume driver here is fundamentally different: a funeral home can't market, staff up, or plan its way to more cases.</p>

<h2>How the per-case model actually works</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Base fee plus a case fee at volume.</strong> The reported structure is a flat monthly base — around $295/month for Halcyon — plus a per-case charge that applies once a funeral home's case volume passes a certain size, alongside a setup fee at onboarding.</li>
  <li><strong>Case volume is not a controllable input.</strong> A landscaping crew count or an ecommerce support-ticket volume both respond, at least partly, to business decisions — hiring, marketing, promotions. Case volume for a funeral home is driven by regional mortality trends the business has no influence over, so a per-case fee bills against a number the operator can't manage down.</li>
  <li><strong>Multi-location operators pay per-location plus per-case.</strong> A funeral home group running several locations typically licenses each location separately, with per-case charges applying at each site independently.</li>
  <li><strong>Case-management workflow is bundled with billing and family portal tools.</strong> Arrangement conferences, obituary publishing, family communication, and merchandise/casket selection often live in the same licensed platform as the case record itself, so the per-case fee effectively taxes every part of that workflow at once.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Where the licensed platform still earns its keep</h2>
<p>Case documentation, state and cemetery filing requirements, and the chain-of-custody record around a case carry real regulatory and legal weight, and the compliance workflow built into Halcyon or Passare reflects years of deathcare-specific requirements. That core case record is the wrong place to look for savings — the cost problem is the per-case fee layered on top of it, not the compliance engine itself.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a family portal replace our case-management and compliance system?</h3>
<p>No — it's built to sync with your existing platform via API. Case filing, chain-of-custody records, and regulatory compliance stay in the system of record; the portal covers family communication and arrangement work that doesn't need that compliance data model.</p>

<h3>Why does our software bill vary month to month when our staffing hasn't changed?</h3>
<p>If your platform charges a per-case fee once volume crosses a threshold, the bill tracks case volume directly — and case volume is driven by regional mortality trends, not a decision your business makes, so the bill can move independent of staffing or marketing effort.</p>

<h3>Is per-case pricing the same as the visit-based billing used in other healthcare verticals?</h3>
<p>The mechanic looks similar — a base fee plus volume-based charges — but the volume driver is different. A physical therapy practice can influence its visit volume through scheduling and referrals; a funeral home has essentially no ability to influence case volume, which makes the cost far harder to plan around.</p>
