<p>Content Hub (HubSpot's CMS and content-marketing product) licenses by seat and by marketing contact tier, layered the same way HubSpot's other Hubs are — the bill grows with headcount and with your contact database size, not necessarily with how much of the content tooling any given person actually uses.</p>

<h2>Who's using a full Content Hub seat, and who isn't</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Content creators and marketing managers</strong> actively building pages, blog posts, and campaigns — the clear fit for a full seat.</li>
  <li><strong>Occasional reviewers and approvers</strong> — stakeholders who review and approve content but don't build it themselves.</li>
  <li><strong>Brand and legal reviewers</strong> who need to see drafts before publish, infrequently, without building anything.</li>
  <li><strong>Sales or ops staff</strong> who occasionally need to check what's published or pull a content asset, not create one.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The contact-tier dimension</h2>
<p>Beyond seats, Content Hub (like other HubSpot Hubs) prices partly by marketing contact volume. A growing content program that drives more traffic and more contacts into the database can push the account into a higher contact tier — the content strategy succeeding is what triggers the cost increase, a version of the same misaligned incentive seen in other per-seat and per-contact licensing models.</p>

<h2>Where a lighter-weight option fits</h2>
<p>For the review and approval workflow specifically — routing drafts to legal, brand, or a manager for sign-off — a <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-portal-development">custom review portal</a> synced to Content Hub's draft status can carry that entire loop without adding a seat for every occasional reviewer. It doesn't replace Content Hub for the people actually building content; it removes the seat requirement for everyone else in the approval chain.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a review portal replace Content Hub's CMS functionality?</h3>
<p>No — content creators keep building and publishing in Content Hub. The portal targets the review/approval step specifically, for people who never need to open the CMS itself.</p>

<h3>Can contact-tier costs be reduced without hurting the content program?</h3>
<p>That's a database hygiene question separate from seat cost — pruning genuinely inactive contacts matters, but the fix for a growing, engaged contact base is usually budgeting for tier growth, not artificially capping it.</p>

<h3>Is this relevant if our content team is small?</h3>
<p>Less so — the seat-cost mismatch matters most once the approval chain includes several occasional reviewers outside the core content team. A two-person content team with no external reviewers has little to gain here.</p>
