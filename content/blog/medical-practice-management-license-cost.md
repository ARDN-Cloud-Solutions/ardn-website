<p>Practice management (PM) software — scheduling, billing, claims, and the administrative workflows around a medical practice — is licensed differently from a patient portal: this is staff-facing software, priced per provider or per staff seat, and it's the internal-operations cost sitting alongside whatever the practice pays for patient-facing tools.</p>

<h2>What drives the license bill</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Per-provider pricing</strong> — the most common model, charging by licensed clinician regardless of how much administrative-staff usage the system actually gets.</li>
  <li><strong>Front-desk and billing staff seats</strong> — often licensed separately from provider seats, and administrative headcount tends to scale with patient volume just as much as clinical headcount does.</li>
  <li><strong>Clearinghouse and claims add-ons</strong> — billing and claims submission modules frequently carry their own per-transaction or per-seat fees on top of the base PM license.</li>
  <li><strong>Multi-location surcharges</strong> — practices with more than one location often pay a per-location premium in addition to per-seat costs.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How this differs from the patient portal cost problem</h2>
<p>This is the staff-facing half of the equation — the practice's own scheduling, billing, and claims workflows — not the patient-facing self-service side. Both scale with growth, but for different reasons: patient-facing tools scale with patient volume, and PM software scales with provider and administrative headcount. See <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/blog/healthcare-patient-portal-per-seat-cost">patient portal software: per-seat cost vs. custom build</a> for the patient-facing comparison.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does replacing PM software mean re-credentialing with payers?</h3>
<p>No — payer credentialing is tied to the practice and providers, not to the software used to submit claims. A custom build integrates with the practice's existing clearinghouse connections rather than requiring new payer relationships.</p>

<h3>Is per-provider pricing ever the better option?</h3>
<p>For a small, single-location practice with few providers, a SaaS PM tool's per-provider cost can be reasonable. The case for a custom build strengthens as provider count, locations, or workflow complexity grow.</p>

<h3>What about EHR integration if PM and EHR are separate systems?</h3>
<p>A custom PM build is scoped to integrate with whichever EHR the practice already runs, through its API — this is a build-time requirement, addressed during scoping rather than left as a gap.</p>
