<p>Most association management systems (AMS) sell member-facing portal access as an add-on priced by active member count, by portal login, or by a tier tied to total membership size. That's the opposite of what an association wants: portal adoption going up is the goal, and a pricing model that charges more for higher adoption fights against it. Here's what a purpose-built, flat-fee member portal actually contains and how it's built.</p>

<h2>What goes in the portal</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Dues and renewal status</strong> — members see what's owed and pay online, synced back to the AMS as the record of truth.</li>
  <li><strong>Event registration</strong> — chapter and national event sign-up without a separate ticketing tool's per-registration fee.</li>
  <li><strong>Member directory</strong> — searchable, with privacy controls members manage themselves.</li>
  <li><strong>Digital membership card / ID</strong> — for chapters and associations that use member ID for benefits or event check-in.</li>
  <li><strong>Document library</strong> — bylaws, certificates, CE credit records, whatever the association's members repeatedly request from staff.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How it connects to your AMS</h2>
<p>The portal is a client-facing front end synced live to your existing AMS through its API — the AMS stays the system of record for membership data, dues, and history. Staff keep working in the AMS exactly as before; the portal changes what members see, not what your team runs on.</p>

<p>This is the member-facing side of the equation, distinct from staff seat costs inside the AMS itself. <h2>Rollout: what a build actually looks like</h2>
<p>A member portal build starts with mapping which AMS fields and workflows the portal needs (dues status, event catalog, directory fields, document types), then building against the AMS's API in parallel with staff continuing to use the AMS unchanged. Cutover for members happens once the portal is validated against real account data — not a hard switch that risks member-facing downtime.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a flat-fee portal mean unlimited members?</h3>
<p>Yes — that's the structural difference from per-seat or per-active-member pricing. The fee is set for the build and its ongoing operation, not for a headcount that has to be tracked and re-billed as membership changes.</p>

<h3>Can it handle multiple chapters or membership tiers?</h3>
<p>Yes, when it's built to your structure from the start — chapter-specific views, tiered benefit visibility, and role-based directory access are scoping decisions made up front, not limitations of the flat-fee model.</p>

<h3>What happens to our AMS if we add a custom portal?</h3>
<p>Nothing changes about it. The AMS keeps running as your system of record; the portal reads and writes to it through the API. Staff workflows inside the AMS are unaffected.</p>
