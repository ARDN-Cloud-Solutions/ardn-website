<p>If your CRM bill climbs every time you hire, you're almost certainly paying full per-seat prices for people who barely touch the platform. Per-seat pricing is built around the power user — the rep who lives in the CRM all day. Everyone lighter than that is where the overpayment quietly piles up.</p>

<p>The good news: you don't need to rip out Salesforce or HubSpot to fix it. You just need to know which users are "light," and what to do with them. Here's a practical audit you can run this week.</p>

<h2>What is a "light" CRM user?</h2>

<p>A light user touches the CRM for a narrow slice of work but gets billed the same (or nearly the same) as someone who uses the whole thing. Common examples:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Field sellers and dealers</strong> who check inventory or update an order status a couple of times a day.</li>
  <li><strong>Operations and back-office staff</strong> who update records or move a case along, but never build reports or run pipeline.</li>
  <li><strong>Partners and vendors</strong> who submit and track requests — external users sitting on internal seats by default.</li>
  <li><strong>Customers</strong> who just need to see status or make a request.</li>
</ul>

<p>In most mid-market organizations, these people <em>outnumber</em> the power users. So the overpayment isn't a rounding error in the corner of your license count — it's spread across the majority of it.</p>

<h2>The 4-step light-user audit</h2>

<h3>1. Pull login and feature-usage data</h3>
<p>Most CRMs expose last-login dates and, at higher tiers, feature-level usage. Export it. You're looking for two groups: people who almost never log in (dormant) and people who log in but only ever touch one or two screens (light).</p>

<h3>2. Separate "dormant" from "light"</h3>
<p>Dormant users — left the company, changed roles, provisioned "just in case" — are the fastest win, because deactivating them changes nothing for anyone active. Light users are trickier: they're genuinely working, just not using much of what you're paying for.</p>

<h3>3. Map each light user to what they actually do</h3>
<p>For each light user or group, write down the handful of tasks they perform. If that list fits on a sticky note, they don't need a full platform seat — they need a focused tool that does exactly those tasks.</p>

<h3>4. Choose the right lever for each group</h3>
<p>There are three honest ways to cut the bill, and you'll usually use more than one:</p>

<h2>Why light users are the biggest opportunity</h2>

<p>A license audit finds seats you're not using and reclaims them — useful, but it can't shrink seats that <em>are</em> being used, just lightly. That second group is where the durable savings sit, because per-seat fees recur and grow with headcount forever, while a purpose-built <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-portal-development">custom portal</a> costs the same whether 10 light users are on it or 400.</p>

<p>To put rough numbers on it: a full Sales or Service Cloud seat lists around $150–$165/user/month, and even limited-access or login-based tiers still bill per person. (Those are publicly listed ranges, not a quote — confirm your own rates.) Multiply that by dozens of light users and the annual figure gets serious fast.</p>

<h2>Cut the bill without switching CRMs</h2>

<p>The key point: this isn't "leave Salesforce." Your CRM stays the system of record. A custom portal sits alongside it, syncs through its API in real time, and gives light users a clean tool built for their job — on one flat fee instead of a license each. Your power users never notice a thing.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>How do I find light users if I don't have feature-usage reporting?</h3>
<p>Start with login frequency and role. Anyone in a field, ops, partner, or customer-facing role who logs in occasionally is a strong candidate. A short conversation with team leads about what those people actually do in the CRM fills in the rest.</p>

<h3>Isn't moving users to a cheaper license tier enough?</h3>
<p>It helps, but cheaper tiers still bill per person and cap what you can build for them. You're renting a smaller box, not escaping the per-seat model. A flat-fee portal breaks the link between cost and headcount entirely.</p>

<h3>Will a portal keep my CRM data accurate?</h3>
<p>Yes. It reads and writes the same CRM records in real time, so there's one source of truth — the portal is a tailored window into the data you already trust, not a second database that drifts out of sync.</p>
