<p>Most CRM contract problems get discovered at the next renewal, when it's too late to negotiate them away. This is a vendor-agnostic checklist of the specific clauses worth reading closely before signing — regardless of which CRM you're on.</p>

<h2>Auto-renewal and notice period</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Does the contract auto-renew by default, and what's the notice period to opt out?</li>
  <li>Is the notice window long enough to actually run an evaluation of alternatives before it closes?</li>
  <li>Does auto-renewal lock in the current seat count, or does it reset to a higher committed minimum?</li>
</ul>

<h2>Price escalators</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Is there a built-in annual price increase, and is it capped or open-ended?</li>
  <li>Does the increase apply to existing seats, or only to new ones added after renewal?</li>
  <li>Are add-on modules subject to separate, uncapped price increases?</li>
</ul>

<h2>Seat-count minimums and true-ups</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Is there a minimum seat commitment that doesn't shrink if actual usage drops?</li>
  <li>How does a mid-term "true-up" for added users work, and how does it differ from the renewal-time rate?</li>
  <li>Can seats be reduced before the next renewal, or only at renewal itself?</li>
</ul>

<h2>Feature gating and tier lock-in</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Are features you currently rely on being moved to a higher tier at renewal?</li>
  <li>Does the new contract quietly bundle add-ons you didn't ask for into the base price?</li>
</ul>

<h2>Data export and exit terms</h2>
<ul>
  <li>What does the contract guarantee about data export format and timeline if you leave?</li>
  <li>Is there a fee for data export or an extended "read-only" access period after cancellation?</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>How is this different from a Salesforce-specific renewal negotiation guide?</h3>
<p>This checklist covers contract clauses that apply to any CRM vendor's paperwork. Salesforce-specific renewal tactics — timing, discount structures unique to that platform — are covered separately for readers on that specific CRM.</p>

<h3>Should we push back on every red flag we find?</h3>
<p>Prioritize the ones with real financial exposure — uncapped escalators and seat minimums with no true-down — over cosmetic issues. Vendors have more room to move on some clauses than others, and a focused ask lands better than a long list.</p>

<h3>What if the vendor won't remove a clause we flag?</h3>
<p>That's useful information on its own — it tells you how much leverage you actually have at this renewal, and whether the alternative (reducing seat dependency structurally) is worth accelerating.</p>
