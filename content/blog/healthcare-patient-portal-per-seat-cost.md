<p>Patient portal functionality — appointment requests, secure messaging, records access, intake forms — is commonly bundled into EHR and practice-management platforms as a module priced per provider, per active patient, or per portal login. As a practice grows its patient base, the exact metric the portal is meant to encourage (more patients engaging self-service) is the one driving the bill up.</p>

<h2>Where the per-seat model breaks down for patient-facing tools</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Provider-based pricing</strong> penalizes practices for adding clinicians, even when the portal's actual usage is driven by patient volume, not provider count.</li>
  <li><strong>Active-patient pricing</strong> ties cost directly to portal adoption succeeding — the same misaligned incentive seen in other client-facing licensing models.</li>
  <li><strong>Feature bundling</strong> often forces a practice into a higher EHR tier just to unlock portal features it needs, even if the rest of that tier goes unused.</li>
</ul>

<p>A <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-portal-development">custom patient portal</a> synced to the practice's existing EHR or practice-management system delivers appointment requests, secure messaging, and records access on a flat monthly fee — the practice's EHR stays the clinical system of record.</p>

<h2>Compliance and access control still come first</h2>
<p>Nothing about moving to a flat-fee model changes the access-control and data-handling standards a patient-facing tool has to meet. A custom build is scoped to the same standard the practice already applies to protected health information, not a generic vendor default — this is a design requirement from day one of the build, not an afterthought.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a custom patient portal replace our EHR?</h3>
<p>No — it's a patient-facing front end synced to your existing EHR or practice-management system through its API. Clinical documentation and records stay exactly where they are.</p>

<h3>Can it handle appointment scheduling across multiple providers or locations?</h3>
<p>Yes, when scoped to your specific scheduling rules and provider/location structure during the build — that's a requirements decision, not a limitation of the flat-fee model.</p>

<h3>Why not just use the EHR's built-in portal and eat the cost?</h3>
<p>For some practices that's the right call, especially at low patient volume. The math shifts as patient volume or provider count grows — that's the comparison worth running before renewal, not after.</p>
