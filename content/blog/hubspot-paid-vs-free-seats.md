<p>HubSpot markets free CRM seats prominently, and for a small team that's genuinely useful. Past a certain size, the free-seat story gets more complicated: certain workflows, permissions, and reporting views are gated to paid seats, and teams often end up buying more paid seats than they need simply because nobody mapped who requires which features.</p>

<h2>What a free seat actually includes</h2>
<p>Free seats get core CRM record access — contacts, companies, basic deals — plus limited use of some tools. What they don't get varies by hub and tier: certain custom reporting, some automation actions, and specific permission sets are paid-seat gated. The gate isn't always obvious until someone hits it mid-task.</p>

<h2>The three groups worth separating</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Power users</strong> — sales reps, marketers, service agents who build sequences, run reports, and configure automation daily. They need paid seats; the features are the job.</li>
  <li><strong>Light record-viewers</strong> — managers checking pipeline, support staff looking up a contact, ops staff pulling a status. Free seats often cover this, if the permission model lines up.</li>
  <li><strong>Occasional external or narrow-task users</strong> — someone who needs one specific view or action repeatedly, but doesn't fit either HubSpot seat type cleanly. This is the group where a seat of either kind is the wrong tool.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Where teams overpay</h2>
<p>The common pattern: a manager or occasional user hits one paid-seat-gated feature, and rather than working around it, the team upgrades that person to a paid seat "just in case" — for a feature they'll use once a quarter. Multiply that across a growing team and paid seats creep well past the group that actually needs them daily.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>How do we find out who's on a paid seat but rarely uses paid features?</h3>
<p>HubSpot's user activity and permission reports show last-login and feature usage per seat — cross-reference against paid-seat-gated feature usage to find candidates for downgrade.</p>

<h3>Will downgrading someone to a free seat break their workflows?</h3>
<p>Only if they're actively using a paid-gated feature. Confirm actual usage, not assumed usage, before downgrading — that's the same freeze-before-deactivate caution that applies to any license change.</p>

<h3>Does a custom portal replace HubSpot for these users?</h3>
<p>No — HubSpot stays the system of record. The portal is a purpose-built, synced front end for users whose need is narrower than either HubSpot seat type assumes.</p>
