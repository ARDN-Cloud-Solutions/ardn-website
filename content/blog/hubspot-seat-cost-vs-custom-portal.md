<p>HubSpot's pricing is simpler than Salesforce's, but the underlying math is the same: every user costs more, every month, forever. For power users running full sales and marketing workflows, that's a fair trade for a powerful platform. For everyone else, it's worth comparing against a flat-fee alternative.</p>

<h2>What a HubSpot seat gets you</h2>
<p>A full HubSpot seat is genuinely worth it for the people who use the platform's depth — sequences, forecasting, workflow automation, reporting. If that's the job, HubSpot's native tooling is hard to beat and the per-seat cost is a reasonable trade for the capability.</p>

<h2>Where the per-seat model breaks down</h2>
<p>The problem shows up with light users: field sellers checking an order, ops staff updating a status, partners submitting a request, customers viewing account info. These people don't need HubSpot's depth — but on a per-seat model, they're billed close to what a power user pays, or they get squeezed into a limited role that still costs per person.</p>

<h2>What a custom portal changes</h2>
<p>A <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-portal-development">custom portal</a> built for exactly those light-user workflows syncs live with HubSpot through its API, so the CRM data stays accurate and current — but the cost structure changes completely. It's one flat monthly fee whether 10 people use it or 500. As the light-user group grows (which it usually does, faster than the power-user group), the economics tilt further toward the flat fee.</p>

<h2>A simple way to decide</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Runs sequences, builds reports, lives in HubSpot daily?</strong> Keep the seat.</li>
  <li><strong>Touches HubSpot for a narrow set of tasks a few times a week?</strong> Model the portal option.</li>
  <li><strong>External user (partner, customer) accessing limited data?</strong> Portal, almost always — you shouldn't be paying an internal-tier seat for someone outside the company.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Can a custom portal and HubSpot run side by side?</h3>
<p>Yes — that's the normal setup. Power users keep HubSpot seats; light and external users work in a portal that reads and writes the same HubSpot records in real time. One source of truth, two access models matched to actual need.</p>

<h3>Is this only relevant for HubSpot's higher tiers?</h3>
<p>No. Even Starter-tier seats add up once you're assigning them to dozens of light users who don't need HubSpot's core sales/marketing features at all — the fix is removing them from per-seat licensing entirely, not just picking a cheaper tier.</p>

<h3>Do we lose reporting visibility if light users move to a portal?</h3>
<p>No — the portal writes back to the same HubSpot records, so anything a light user updates still shows up in your HubSpot reporting and pipeline. You're changing the front end for that user group, not the system of record.</p>
