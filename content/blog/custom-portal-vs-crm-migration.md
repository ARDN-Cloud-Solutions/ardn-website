<p>When CRM costs get painful enough, migrating to a cheaper platform entirely is one option on the table. It's also the most disruptive one. Before committing to a full migration, it's worth understanding what a custom portal solves without that disruption — and where migration is genuinely the right call instead.</p>

<h2>What a full CRM migration actually involves</h2>
<p>Moving from one CRM to another means re-mapping every custom field, workflow, integration, and report; retraining every user on a new interface; and living through a data-migration project with real risk of errors, downtime, and lost historical context. Even a well-run migration is a multi-month undertaking, and the new platform typically still bills per seat — you're changing vendors, not changing the pricing model.</p>

<h2>What a custom portal solves instead</h2>
<p>A <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-portal-development">custom portal</a> doesn't touch your CRM at all — it syncs to it live through the API and gives specific user groups (light internal users, partners, customers) exactly the access they need, on one flat monthly fee. Your CRM stays the system of record, your power users keep working exactly as they do today, and only the light-user population moves off per-seat pricing.</p>

<h2>When migration is actually the right call</h2>
<p>If your core team's workflow is fundamentally mismatched with your current CRM — not a cost problem but a fit problem — migration can be worth the disruption regardless of price. Similarly, if you're locked into a platform with no reasonable path to reduce seat costs for your power users specifically, and a genuinely better-fit platform exists, that's a real reason to migrate.</p>

<h2>When a portal is the better move</h2>
<p>If the actual cost driver is a large and growing population of light, external, or occasional users — not your core sales, service, or ops team — migrating the whole CRM doesn't target the problem. It disrupts everyone to fix a cost that only a subset of users are causing. A portal fixes exactly that subset, with none of the migration risk to your core team's workflow. See the numbers behind this in <a href="/blog/salesforce-platform-license-vs-custom-portal">Salesforce platform license vs. custom portal</a>, or model your own situation with the <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/savings-calculator">savings calculator</a>.</p>

<p>Either path is a response to the same root issue covered in <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/reduce-crm-licensing-costs">how to cut CRM licensing costs</a> — the question is just which lever fits your actual cost driver.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Can we do both — migrate the core CRM and add a portal for light users?</h3>
<p>Yes, they're not mutually exclusive. Some teams migrate the core platform for fit reasons and still add a portal afterward for the light-user population, since migration alone rarely fixes per-seat cost for that group.</p>

<h3>Does a portal delay a migration decision, or replace the need for one?</h3>
<p>It can do either. For some teams it removes enough of the cost pressure that migration stops being necessary. For others, it's a way to control light-user cost now while a separate, unhurried migration decision plays out for the core team.</p>

<h3>How long does standing up a portal typically take compared to a migration?</h3>
<p>A portal build is typically measured in weeks; a full CRM migration is typically measured in months. The scope is genuinely different — a portal is a new front end synced to your existing system, not a system replacement.</p>
