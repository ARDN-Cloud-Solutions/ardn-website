<p>CRM licensing is one of the few line items that grows every time you hire — and for most mid-market teams, a big chunk of it is spent on people who barely use the platform. The good news is you have more levers than "call the vendor and beg for a discount." Here are seven that actually move the number, roughly in order of effort-to-payoff.</p>

<h2>1. Reclaim dormant and duplicate seats</h2>
<p>The fastest win, because it changes nothing for anyone active. Export last-login data and flag accounts that haven't logged in for 60–90 days, plus duplicates from role changes and departures. On Salesforce, <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/license-guard">License Guard</a> automates that scan continuously so seats don't quietly pile back up between audits.</p>

<h2>2. Right-size users to the correct license tier</h2>
<p>Not everyone needs a full Sales or Service Cloud seat. CRMs sell platform, limited-access, and login-based tiers for a fraction of the price. Match each user to the smallest tier that still covers their job. It's real savings — just remember these tiers still bill per person, so it's a discount, not an escape from per-seat pricing.</p>

<h2>3. Move light users to a flat-fee custom portal</h2>
<p>This is the biggest structural lever. Sellers, ops staff, partners, and customers who touch the CRM for a handful of tasks don't need a platform seat at all — they need a focused tool. A <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-portal-development">custom portal</a> gives them exactly that, wired live into your CRM, on one flat fee no matter how many users you add. Per-seat fees grow with headcount forever; a flat fee doesn't. (See the full breakdown in our guide to <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/reduce-crm-licensing-costs">cutting CRM licensing costs</a>.)</p>

<h2>4. Consolidate overlapping tools</h2>
<p>Shadow IT and tool sprawl mean you're often paying for two or three products that overlap with the CRM or each other. Map what each tool actually does; if a workflow can live in one system, cancel the others. Fewer contracts also means fewer per-seat meters running.</p>

<h2>5. Renegotiate at renewal — with usage data in hand</h2>
<p>Vendors negotiate hardest when you arrive with evidence. Bring your seat-utilization numbers and a credible alternative (a right-sized tier or a portal plan) to the renewal. "We're moving 120 light users to a portal unless the per-seat price changes" is a very different conversation than "can we get a discount."</p>

<h2>6. Automate offboarding so seats free up instantly</h2>
<p>Every departure should trigger automatic deactivation. Manual offboarding is how orgs end up paying for ex-employees for months. Tie license removal to your HR or identity system so it happens the day someone leaves.</p>

<h2>7. Stop paying internal seats for external users</h2>
<p>Partners, vendors, and customers frequently end up on internal CRM licenses by default — the most expensive way possible to give an outsider limited access. Move them to a portal or an appropriate external tier so you're never paying a full internal seat for someone outside the company.</p>

<h2>Which lever should you start with?</h2>
<p>Run them roughly in order: reclaim first (free), then right-size, then move the structurally-light users to a portal for the durable win. Most teams combine several. <h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Do any of these require leaving Salesforce or HubSpot?</h3>
<p>No. Every lever here keeps your CRM as the system of record. Even the portal option syncs live with your CRM through its API — your power users keep working exactly as they do today.</p>

<h3>How much can a mid-market team realistically save?</h3>
<p>It depends on how many light and dormant users you have and your negotiated seat rate, so there's no single number. The mechanism is what matters: reclaiming and right-sizing trims the existing bill, and moving light users to a flat fee stops the bill from scaling with headcount. Use the savings calculator for your own figures.</p>

<h3>What's the difference between a cheaper license tier and a custom portal?</h3>
<p>A cheaper tier still bills per person and limits what you can build for that user. A custom portal is a flat monthly fee regardless of user count and is shaped to your exact workflow — so it tends to win as user counts grow.</p>
