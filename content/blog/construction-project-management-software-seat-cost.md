<p>Construction project management software doesn't price uniformly across the category — some enterprise platforms bill by annual construction volume with unlimited users, while much of the mid-market tier still charges per named user, by role (project manager, superintendent, admin, read-only). For a general contractor on one of those per-user platforms, the bill scales with office and field headcount in a way volume-based pricing doesn't.</p>

<h2>Where per-seat pricing actually shows up</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Office and field staff mix.</strong> Project engineers, superintendents, estimators, and back-office admin each typically need a named seat, priced by role tier — full PM access costs more than a read-only or field-limited seat.</li>
  <li><strong>Seasonal and part-time supers.</strong> Construction staffing flexes with active project count; a per-seat platform doesn't flex down automatically when a super rolls off between projects, and re-adding seats each season adds administrative overhead on top of the cost.</li>
  <li><strong>Occasional office viewers.</strong> Accounting, ownership, and back-office staff who need to check project status or budgets occasionally often require the same paid seat tier as a daily user of the system.</li>
  <li><strong>Module and add-on stacking.</strong> Estimating, scheduling, safety, and financials are frequently separate modules or add-ons layered on top of the base per-seat price, each with its own line item.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Where volume-based or flat pricing fits better</h2>
<p>Larger contractors already on a volume-based enterprise platform, or firms whose crew and office headcount genuinely stays proportional to revenue, may already have pricing that scales sensibly for their business. The mismatch is sharpest for mid-market GCs on a per-user platform whose office and field headcount doesn't track cleanly with revenue — bidding season, multi-project overlap, and seasonal supers all add seats independent of top-line growth.</p>

<p>A pre-built platform earns its cost when a GC genuinely needs its full breadth — estimating, scheduling, safety, and financials all in one system, maintained by the vendor. <h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Is volume-based pricing always cheaper than per-seat pricing?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily — it depends on your specific headcount, project volume, and how the two models compare at your actual scale. Run both against your real numbers rather than assuming either direction.</p>

<h3>Does a custom project portal replace our estimating or accounting software?</h3>
<p>No — it typically sits alongside those systems as a project-visibility and workflow layer, syncing data via API rather than replacing specialized estimating or accounting tools.</p>

<h3>Is this worth evaluating mid-contract, or only at renewal?</h3>
<p>Renewal is the natural checkpoint, but if seasonal seat churn or module add-ons are adding up now, it's worth pricing out sooner rather than waiting for the contract date.</p>
