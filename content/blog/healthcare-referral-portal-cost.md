<p>Referral coordination sits between practices — a primary care office referring to a specialist, a hospital system routing to affiliated providers, a specialty group tracking referral status and outcomes. Referral management software licenses this differently from a patient portal or practice-management system: it's staff-facing software used by both the referring and receiving side, and the seat count spans multiple organizations, not just one.</p>

<h2>Where referral-tracking costs add up</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Per-user licensing on both ends.</strong> Staff at the referring practice and the receiving specialist both typically need seats, doubling the license footprint for a single referral relationship.</li>
  <li><strong>Per-location or per-practice fees</strong> for referral networks spanning multiple sites or affiliated practices.</li>
  <li><strong>Status-tracking and communication add-ons</strong> — secure messaging between referring and receiving staff is sometimes a separate module.</li>
  <li><strong>Network growth cost.</strong> Adding a new referral partner to the network often means adding a new set of licensed seats on their side too.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why this differs from patient-facing or internal PM tools</h2>
<p>This isn't the patient self-service problem, and it isn't the internal scheduling-and-billing problem — it's coordination across organizational boundaries, where the "users" needing access span practices that don't share a single IT budget or procurement decision. That's a structurally different licensing problem than a single practice's patient portal or PM software. <h2>What to scope carefully before building</h2>
<p>Referral coordination across organizational lines has to work for staff who aren't on your payroll — the receiving specialist's front desk, an affiliated practice's care coordinator. Access and permissions need to be scoped per organization from day one, not treated as an afterthought, and EHR integration on both ends needs to be confirmed before committing to a build timeline.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Who pays for the portal if referral partners are on the other side?</h3>
<p>Typically the initiating practice or health system funds the build, since they're the one replacing licensed seats; partner practices get scoped access without their own subscription.</p>

<h3>Does this replace our EHR's referral module?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily — it can be built to integrate with your existing EHR's referral data rather than replace it, depending on how much of the coordination workflow the EHR module already covers well.</p>

<h3>How does this handle HIPAA and cross-organization data sharing?</h3>
<p>Access control and data-sharing agreements between organizations are a design requirement scoped at the start of the build, matching the same compliance standard the practices already apply to protected health information.</p>
