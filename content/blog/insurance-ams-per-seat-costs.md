<p>Agency management systems (AMS platforms) are the system of record for most insurance agencies — policies, clients, carriers, commissions all live there. Like most core business software, they're typically licensed per user. That's a fair model for producers and CSRs who live in the system daily. It gets expensive fast once you look at everyone else who touches it.</p>

<h2>Who's actually using a full AMS seat?</h2>
<p>In most agencies, full AMS seats break down into a few groups:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Producers and CSRs</strong> who work in the AMS all day — quoting, servicing, managing renewals.</li>
  <li><strong>Back-office and accounting staff</strong> who touch a narrower slice — commission reconciliation, billing, reporting.</li>
  <li><strong>Part-time and seasonal staff</strong> brought on for renewal season or specific campaigns.</li>
  <li><strong>Carrier and MGA contacts</strong> who occasionally need to check status on a submission or claim.</li>
  <li><strong>Clients</strong> who just want to see a policy document or request a certificate.</li>
</ul>
<p>Only the first group typically needs the full system. Everyone else is a candidate for a lighter-weight, cheaper access model — if the AMS offers one, and if it fits the exact workflow.</p>

<h2>The two structural options</h2>

<h3>Right-size within the AMS</h3>
<p>Some AMS platforms offer limited or view-only license tiers at a lower per-seat rate. Worth checking with your vendor — it's a real, if partial, saving. The seat count still grows with headcount, though, just at a lower rate per person.</p>

<h3>Move light users to a purpose-built portal</h3>
<p>For the groups whose work is genuinely narrow — a carrier checking submission status, a client requesting a certificate, seasonal staff doing one task — a <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-portal-development">custom portal</a> synced live to your AMS via its API gives them exactly what they need, without an AMS seat at all. It's one flat monthly fee regardless of how many carriers, clients, or seasonal staff use it, which matters in an industry where client and carrier counts grow independently of internal headcount.</p>

<h2>Why this matters more as an agency grows</h2>
<p>Client count, carrier relationships, and seasonal staffing all tend to scale faster than the core producer/CSR headcount at a growing agency. If every one of those touchpoints requires an AMS seat, the license bill grows disconnected from the size of your actual sales team. A flat-fee portal for those groups keeps the cost tied to what you're building, not to how many people need to check a status.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Do we have to migrate off our current AMS to do this?</h3>
<p>No. A custom portal is built to sync with your existing AMS through its API — it's an additional, purpose-built front end for specific user groups, not a replacement for your core system.</p>

<h3>Does this work for independent agencies as well as larger ones?</h3>
<p>The mechanism scales down as well as up — the savings are proportional to how many non-producer touchpoints (clients, carriers, seasonal staff) your agency has relative to core seats.</p>

<h3>What about compliance and data security for client/carrier access?</h3>
<p>A custom portal can be built with the same access controls and audit requirements your agency already applies to client and carrier data — the portal doesn't bypass compliance, it's built around it from the start.</p>
