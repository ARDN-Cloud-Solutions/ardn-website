<p>Transportation management systems built for freight brokers, carriers, and shippers — the McLeod/Samsara/project44 category — price differently from generic field-service or CRM software: often a mix of per-user seats for dispatch/ops staff and per-shipment or per-load volume tiers. For a growing brokerage or carrier, both dimensions climb at once as the business scales.</p>

<h2>Where the TMS bill comes from</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Per-user seats for dispatch and ops.</strong> Load planners, dispatchers, and back-office ops staff typically each need a named license, priced by role tier (dispatcher vs. read-only vs. admin).</li>
  <li><strong>Shipment or load-volume tiers.</strong> Many TMS platforms layer a volume-based fee on top of seats — priced by loads tendered, shipments tracked, or freight under management — so growth in freight volume raises cost independent of headcount.</li>
  <li><strong>EDI and integration connections.</strong> Connecting to carrier networks, load boards, and customer EDI feeds is frequently billed per connection or per integration, adding up quickly for brokers working with many carrier partners.</li>
  <li><strong>Driver/carrier-facing app licenses.</strong> Mobile apps for drivers or owner-operators to see loads and submit paperwork often carry their own per-user fee, separate from office staff seats.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why logistics teams feel this compounding effect</h2>
<p>A freight brokerage that grows both its ops headcount and its load volume in the same growth period is paying on two axes simultaneously — more seats and higher volume tiers — in a way a purely per-seat SaaS tool doesn't. That compounding effect is easy to miss when the initial contract was priced against a smaller starting volume.</p>

<h2>Where the fix actually lives</h2>
<p>An off-the-shelf TMS earns its cost when a broker or carrier genuinely needs its pre-built, broad carrier-network integrations and load-board connections. When the operation's workflow is well understood and the integration set is specific — a known ERP, a known set of carrier EDI partners — a <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-software-development">custom-built platform</a> can deliver the same dispatch, tracking, and driver-facing workflow on a flat monthly fee that doesn't scale with load volume. <h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a custom-built TMS still connect to carrier EDI networks?</h3>
<p>Yes — it's built to your specific carrier and load-board integrations rather than a generic, broad connector library, which is often a better fit when your carrier network is already well defined.</p>

<h3>Can volume-tier pricing be negotiated down?</h3>
<p>Sometimes, at renewal and with real leverage from freight volume history — but the underlying mechanic (cost rising with load volume) typically stays in place even after a negotiated discount.</p>

<h3>Is this different from field service dispatch software?</h3>
<p>Yes — TMS platforms are built for freight brokerage, carrier, and shipment-tracking workflows, while field service dispatch software targets trades and home-services technician scheduling. The pricing mechanics differ accordingly.</p>
