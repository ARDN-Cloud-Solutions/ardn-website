<p>Restaurant software splits into two pricing mechanics that are easy to conflate. Point-of-sale platforms typically bill per terminal or per station, plus a cut of card-processing volume. Labor and scheduling tools, by contrast, tend to bill per location — with employee count used to gate which pricing tier a location qualifies for, rather than as a straight per-employee multiplier.</p>

<h2>How restaurant POS pricing scales</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Per-terminal or per-station software fees.</strong> Each POS terminal or register carries its own monthly software charge, so adding a station for a busy patio or a second counter is a direct line-item increase.</li>
  <li><strong>Card-processing percentage on top.</strong> POS platforms take a cut of processed card volume in addition to the terminal fee — and several vendors in this category no longer publish a fixed processing rate, quoting it per merchant based on volume, ticket size, and card mix.</li>
  <li><strong>Peripheral devices metered separately.</strong> Kitchen display systems, self-service kiosks, and other add-on hardware/software each carry their own per-device fee layered on top of the core POS subscription.</li>
  <li><strong>Some platforms price per location instead of per terminal.</strong> A location-based tier structure changes the terminal-count math — multi-terminal locations may cost the same as single-terminal ones under that model, so the two mechanics aren't interchangeable when comparing vendors.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How labor and scheduling software pricing differs</h2>
<p>Employee scheduling and labor-management tools in this category commonly bill per location per month, with employee headcount functioning as a packaging threshold rather than a linear per-person charge — a location under a certain employee count sits on a lower tier, and crossing that threshold (not adding one more scheduled shift) is what triggers the next tier's price. That's a materially different growth curve than a true per-seat SaaS product, where every added employee is its own incremental cost.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Do restaurant POS platforms charge a fixed card-processing rate?</h3>
<p>Not always — several major platforms in this category have moved to quote-based processing rates set per merchant based on volume, ticket size, and card mix, rather than a single published percentage.</p>

<h3>Does adding employees to a location directly increase labor-software cost?</h3>
<p>Often not linearly — many labor and scheduling tools in this category bill per location with employee count used as a tier-qualification threshold, so cost increases happen at specific headcount boundaries rather than per additional employee.</p>

<h3>Are kitchen display systems and kiosks included in the base POS subscription?</h3>
<p>Typically not — these are commonly metered as separate per-device add-ons on top of the core POS software fee.</p>
