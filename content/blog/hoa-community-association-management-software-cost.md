<p>HOA and community association management platforms — PayHOA, Vantaca, and AppFolio's community-association product are the names that come up most — license by unit count, the same shape as apartment leasing or general property management software. But the buyer here is usually different: a volunteer board or a part-time treasurer running a self-managed community, not a professional property manager with a portfolio. That difference matters because per-unit pricing with a minimum-fee floor hits smaller, self-managed associations hardest.</p>

<h2>How the per-unit model actually works</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Tiered per-unit bands.</strong> PayHOA's reported pricing runs in bands by unit count — around $54/month for 0-25 units, climbing through tiers, with a $275 minimum at 500+ units — so the per-unit rate isn't flat across the customer base; it steps up and down by community size.</li>
  <li><strong>Per-unit rate plus a minimum floor.</strong> AppFolio's community-association pricing is reported around $0.80 per unit, but with a roughly $280/month minimum — meaning any community under about 200 units is effectively paying a higher blended per-unit rate than a larger portfolio would, simply because it hasn't hit the floor's breakeven point.</li>
  <li><strong>Custom-quote-only for larger platforms.</strong> Vantaca doesn't publish pricing at all; deployments are quoted individually and are reported to run more expensive than the transparently-tiered options, typical of the same opaque enterprise-quote pattern seen in other association-management categories.</li>
  <li><strong>Assessment and violation-tracking modules stack separately.</strong> Special-assessment billing, architectural review workflows, and violation tracking are frequently sold as add-on modules on top of the core per-unit license, not bundled in by default.</li>
</ul>

<p>This is a different pain point from the landlord-tenant leasing workflow covered in <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/blog/apartment-leasing-software-per-unit-cost">apartment leasing software per-unit costs</a> — an HOA isn't managing lease applications and rent collection for a landlord, it's managing board governance, assessment billing, and architectural-compliance enforcement for a self-governing community, and the minimum-fee-floor dynamic specifically punishes small associations that a professionally-managed rental portfolio doesn't run into as often.</p>

<h2>Where the licensed platform still earns its keep</h2>
<p>Assessment accounting, reserve-study tracking, and the audit trail around board votes and architectural approvals carry real governance and legal weight for a community association, and the compliance record built into these platforms reflects real association-law-specific work. That core governance ledger isn't where a self-managed board should look for savings.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a resident portal replace our assessment accounting and reserve-study system?</h3>
<p>No — it's built to sync with your existing HOA platform via API. Assessment ledgers, reserve studies, and the governance audit trail stay in the system of record; the portal covers resident communication and requests that don't need that accounting data model.</p>

<h3>Why does a small HOA sometimes pay more per unit than a large one?</h3>
<p>Several platforms apply a minimum-fee floor regardless of unit count, so a community under the breakeven threshold for that floor ends up paying a higher effective per-unit rate than a larger community on the same rate card.</p>

<h3>Is this the same problem as apartment leasing software costs?</h3>
<p>No — leasing software prices per rental unit for a landlord's leasing and rent-collection workflow. HOA software prices per owned unit for a self-governing community's board operations and assessment billing, which is a different workflow with a different buyer.</p>
