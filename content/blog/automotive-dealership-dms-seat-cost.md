<p>A dealer management system (DMS) is the operational backbone of a dealership — sales desking, F&I, service scheduling, parts inventory, and accounting typically all run through one platform. That platform is licensed per named user, and because a dealership's headcount spans several distinct departments each running their own module, the seat count — and the bill — grows faster than it would in a single-department business.</p>

<h2>Why DMS seat costs add up faster than expected</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Module-based licensing across departments.</strong> Sales, service, parts, and F&I are often licensed as separate modules, each with its own per-user rate — a dealership running all four pays for named-user access to each independently.</li>
  <li><strong>Narrow-use roles still need a full seat.</strong> A parts counter employee checking one inventory screen, or a service advisor updating a single job status, typically needs the same module license as a user working the full workflow depth of that module.</li>
  <li><strong>Multi-rooftop dealer groups multiply the count.</strong> Each location's staff needs its own licensed seats, and consolidating reporting across rooftops doesn't reduce the underlying per-user cost at each one.</li>
  <li><strong>A concentrated vendor market.</strong> A small number of DMS providers dominate the space, which historically has meant less competitive pressure on per-seat pricing than in more fragmented software categories.</li>
</ul>
