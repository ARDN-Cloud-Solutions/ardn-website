<p>Marketing Hub's pricing model works differently from Sales Hub or Service Hub. Instead of billing mainly by the number of people who log in, it bills largely by the number of marketing contacts in your database — a base platform fee plus additional cost as your contact count crosses tier thresholds. It's a different growth curve, and it catches teams off guard when a successful campaign or a list import quietly pushes them into the next tier.</p>

<h2>How the contact-tier model works</h2>
<p>Marketing Hub plans come with a starting allotment of marketing contacts included in the base price. Once your active marketing contact count exceeds that allotment, HubSpot bills for additional contact-tier increments on top of the base tier price. Professional and Enterprise base tiers are commonly listed starting in the hundreds to low thousands of dollars per month before contact-tier add-ons — HubSpot updates these figures periodically, so confirm the current numbers on their pricing page rather than treating any figure here as current.</p>

<h2>Why this catches teams off guard</h2>

<h3>List growth is often outside marketing's direct control</h3>
<p>Sales adding leads, a webinar list import, or a product signup flow can all grow the marketing contact count without marketing actively "adding" anyone — but the bill grows either way.</p>

<h3>Non-marketing contacts can get swept in</h3>
<p>HubSpot lets you mark contacts as non-marketing to exclude them from the billable count, but that requires someone actively managing the distinction. Left unmanaged, your marketing contact count — and the bill — includes people who were never actually part of a campaign.</p>

<h3>Tier jumps aren't always proportional</h3>
<p>Crossing a contact-tier threshold can move you to a meaningfully higher monthly cost even if the number of new contacts is small relative to your total list, because tiers are priced in blocks, not per-contact increments.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>What counts as a "marketing contact"?</h3>
<p>Generally, any contact you're actively marketing to — email sends, ads audience sync, and similar. Contacts marked non-marketing are excluded from the billable count even if they still exist in your CRM.</p>

<h3>Does deleting old contacts reduce the bill immediately?</h3>
<p>It reduces your marketing contact count, which is what the tier is based on — but confirm how your specific plan handles mid-cycle reductions, since billing adjustments can lag the count change depending on your contract terms.</p>

<h3>Is there a way to avoid contact-tier pricing entirely?</h3>
<p>Not within HubSpot's Marketing Hub — it's a core part of how the product is priced. The lever available to you is managing which contacts count as "marketing" and keeping the list clean, not avoiding the model itself.</p>
