<p>Client-facing document exchange and portal tools for accounting firms don't share one pricing mechanic — they split across at least three distinct meters. Some tools bill per tax return delivered, others bill per staff seat regardless of how many clients or returns move through the system, and at least one vendor bills on two dimensions simultaneously. Comparing quotes across these tools without knowing which meter is running leads to real budgeting mistakes during tax season, when volume spikes hardest.</p>

<h2>The three pricing mechanics in this category</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Per-return, volume-tiered.</strong> Tax-return delivery and e-signature tools in this category are commonly priced per return processed, with the per-return rate declining as annual volume grows — a mechanic that scales directly with the firm's busiest season rather than its headcount.</li>
  <li><strong>Per-staff-seat, client users typically unlimited.</strong> Several client-portal and document-exchange platforms in this category bill per internal staff user, with client-facing "guest" logins included at no additional per-client charge — the cost driver here is the size of the firm's team, not its client base.</li>
  <li><strong>Hybrid seat-plus-client-record pricing.</strong> At least one vendor in this space combines a per-user staff subscription with a separate client-record count — a free tier up to a set number of client contacts, with additional contacts billed in fixed increments beyond that threshold. That's a meaningfully different growth curve than a pure per-seat or pure per-return model.</li>
  <li><strong>Annual contract structures vary.</strong> Some platforms bill month-to-month per seat; others structure pricing as an annual per-user rate with multi-year discounts — worth clarifying before comparing a monthly quote from one vendor against an annualized quote from another.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why the meter matters more than the sticker price</h2>
<p>A firm evaluating "client portal software" by list price alone is comparing incompatible units. A per-return tool's cost tracks tax-season volume and stays flat the rest of the year; a per-seat tool's cost tracks staff headcount and stays flat regardless of how many returns or clients move through it; a hybrid tool's cost can climb on either axis independently. The right fit depends on whether the firm's real cost driver is staff count, return volume, or total client-relationship count — and that's rarely the same answer for every firm.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Do accounting firm portal tools all charge per staff seat?</h3>
<p>No — some are priced per tax return delivered rather than per seat, which is a materially different cost driver during a firm's busiest season.</p>

<h3>Are client or guest logins usually a separate charge?</h3>
<p>Usually not on per-seat platforms, where client-facing access is commonly included at no additional cost — though at least one vendor in this category does meter client-record count separately once a free threshold is exceeded.</p>

<h3>Which pricing mechanic is cheapest for a small firm with high seasonal volume?</h3>
<p>It depends on the firm's actual mix, but a small team with a sharp tax-season return spike is often better matched to a per-return, volume-tiered tool than a per-seat one, since seat-based cost doesn't fall in the off-season the way per-return cost naturally does.</p>
