<p>Manufacturing ERP systems run planning, finance, inventory, and shop-floor operations from one platform, licensed by named user — typically at a monthly per-user rate that climbs with the modules a given license needs to reach. That model fits back-office finance and planning roles reasonably well. It fits less well on the shop floor, where a large share of licensed users need one narrow function — clocking in a work order, scanning a barcode, confirming a quantity — not the full breadth of what an ERP seat is priced to deliver.</p>

<h2>Where the per-user model strains on the floor</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Machine operators and floor staff.</strong> Confirming a work order or updating a production count is a fraction of what a full ERP seat is licensed for, but many systems still require the same named-user license to do it.</li>
  <li><strong>Module-based pricing across functions.</strong> Finance, planning/MRP, inventory, and shop-floor modules are frequently licensed separately, so a plant needing only shop-floor tracking still often pays a rate comparable to a full planning seat.</li>
  <li><strong>Multi-plant scaling.</strong> Each additional facility multiplies the seat count required for equivalent floor coverage, even when the actual per-plant usage pattern is identical and narrow.</li>
  <li><strong>Occasional supervisory access.</strong> A shift supervisor checking status across stations, without ever building a plan or touching finance, frequently still needs a licensed seat to see it.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Where native ERP still earns its keep</h2>
<p>Planning, finance, and inventory accuracy genuinely benefit from one integrated system of record — the core ERP isn't the problem, and most manufacturers are right to keep it as the source of truth for cost accounting, MRP, and inventory valuation.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a shop-floor interface replace our ERP?</h3>
<p>No — it's built to read from and write to your existing ERP via API. Planning, finance, and inventory management stay in the native system; the interface covers the narrow, high-volume floor interactions that don't need a full seat.</p>

<h3>Does this help across multiple plants?</h3>
<p>Yes — a flat monthly fee means adding facilities or shifts doesn't change the software cost the way per-user, per-module ERP licenses multiply across each additional site.</p>

<h3>Is switching ERP vendors a better fix than this?</h3>
<p>Rarely — most mainstream manufacturing ERPs price by named user in a similar way, so switching typically changes the vendor without solving the underlying floor-seat mismatch.</p>
