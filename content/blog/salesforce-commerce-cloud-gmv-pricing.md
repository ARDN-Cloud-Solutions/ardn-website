<p>Salesforce Commerce Cloud breaks from the per-seat model entirely: it's priced as a percentage of gross merchandise value (GMV) — broadly, merchandise revenue net of tax and shipping — that flows through the platform. There's no per-user license to manage, but there's also no ceiling: the platform fee grows automatically every time revenue does, which makes it a fundamentally different cost conversation than a per-seat CRM.</p>

<h2>How the GMV model actually works</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>A percentage of revenue, not a flat license.</strong> Commerce Cloud's core fee scales with the merchandise value transacted through the storefront — publicly reported estimates suggest the rate varies by edition and negotiated contract, though Salesforce doesn't publish an official rate card, so any specific percentage cited elsewhere should be treated as illustrative, not quoted pricing.</li>
  <li><strong>Success becomes a cost line.</strong> Because the fee is a share of sales, a strong revenue quarter directly increases the platform bill — the incentive structure is the opposite of a flat-fee system, where growth doesn't change what you pay for software.</li>
  <li><strong>Add-ons carry their own GMV-based fees.</strong> Order Management and other add-on modules are commonly priced with their own separate GMV-based charge, stacking on top of the core platform fee rather than being included in it.</li>
  <li><strong>Premium support scales with the license fee.</strong> Success plans like Premier are commonly priced as a percentage of the license fee itself, so a larger GMV-driven bill also inflates the cost of support wrapped around it.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Where GMV pricing genuinely fits</h2>
<p>For high-growth or highly seasonal storefronts, a revenue-share model avoids overpaying for platform capacity during slow periods — the bill contracts along with sales in the way a fixed per-seat or per-server license never would. That flexibility is real, and it's the honest case for GMV pricing over a flat fee for a business whose revenue swings widely month to month.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does Salesforce publish its official GMV percentage rates?</h3>
<p>Not as a public rate card — Commerce Cloud pricing is typically quoted per contract based on edition and negotiated terms, so any specific percentage you see cited publicly should be treated as a third-party estimate, not confirmed pricing.</p>

<h3>Does GMV pricing include Order Management and other add-ons?</h3>
<p>Usually not — those are commonly priced with their own separate GMV-based or usage-based fee on top of the core platform charge, so the total bill is often more than the headline platform percentage alone.</p>

<h3>Is switching to a flat-fee model an all-or-nothing decision?</h3>
<p>No — a custom-built layer can run alongside Commerce Cloud for specific storefront features or back-office workflows without requiring a full platform migration, letting you shift only the parts where a percentage-of-revenue fee no longer makes sense.</p>
