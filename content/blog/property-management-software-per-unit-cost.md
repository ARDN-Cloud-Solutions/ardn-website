<p>Property management platforms in the AppFolio/Buildium/RentManager category price primarily per unit under management — a mechanic that's easy to underestimate for a portfolio actively acquiring properties, since the software cost grows with every door added, on top of whatever per-user seats the office staff also carry.</p>

<h2>How per-unit pricing actually stacks up</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Per-unit or per-door monthly fees.</strong> The core recurring charge scales with total units managed, so a portfolio that grows from 500 to 1,500 doors triples that line item, independent of staff headcount.</li>
  <li><strong>Per-user seats on top.</strong> Property managers, leasing agents, and maintenance coordinators often also need individual named-user access, layered on top of the per-unit fee rather than replacing it.</li>
  <li><strong>Tenant/owner portal add-ons.</strong> Online rent payment portals, owner-facing reporting dashboards, and maintenance request systems are frequently sold as separate modules with their own per-unit or flat add-on fee.</li>
  <li><strong>Screening and payment processing fees.</strong> Tenant screening, background checks, and payment processing frequently carry per-transaction fees on top of the base platform subscription.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why portfolio growth compounds this cost</h2>
<p>Unlike a typical CRM where seat count grows roughly with staff hiring, a property management platform's core fee grows with every unit acquired — and unit growth in property management often outpaces staffing growth, since a lean team can manage a meaningfully larger portfolio with good software. That means the per-unit fee often becomes the fastest-growing line item in the operating budget as a portfolio scales.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does per-unit pricing apply to vacant units too?</h3>
<p>Policies vary by vendor — some platforms bill only occupied units, others bill total units under management regardless of occupancy. Confirm this specifically before signing, since it materially changes the effective cost during vacancy periods.</p>

<h3>Can a custom portal replace the whole property management platform?</h3>
<p>Not usually wholesale — accounting and trust-fund handling in property management carries regulatory requirements many portfolios prefer to keep on established, audited software. A custom build more commonly replaces the tenant/owner-facing layer while the core accounting system stays in place.</p>

<h3>Who should own the per-unit cost review?</h3>
<p>Whoever owns the software vendor relationship, ideally reviewed at each portfolio-size milestone (not just at renewal), since per-unit cost growth is continuous rather than a once-a-year event.</p>
