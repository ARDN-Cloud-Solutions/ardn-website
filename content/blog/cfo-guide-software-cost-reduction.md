<p>Software spend is one of the few line items that grows quietly, department by department, until someone finally adds it all up. For a CFO, the hard part usually isn't finding the number — it's cutting it without a fight, and without breaking something a team genuinely relies on. Here's a framework that starts with the least disruptive levers and only escalates when the math justifies it.</p>

<h2>1. Get one real number first</h2>
<p>Before proposing any cut, consolidate every software contract — CRM, helpdesk, HR, finance, project tools — into one list with seat counts, renewal dates, and per-seat rates. Most orgs have never seen this in one place, and the total is usually higher than any single department realizes, because no one owns the cross-departmental view.</p>

<h2>2. Separate one-time cleanup from structural fixes</h2>
<p>Not every dollar saved is the same kind of saving:</p>
<table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Lever</th><th>Type</th><th>Recurs automatically?</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Reclaim dormant/duplicate seats</td><td>One-time</td><td>No — needs a recurring audit to stay clean</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Right-size license tiers</td><td>Recurring, but still per-seat</td><td>Grows again with headcount</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Renegotiate at renewal</td><td>Recurring, but time-boxed to contract cycles</td><td>Resets each renewal</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Move light users to flat-fee tools</td><td>Structural</td><td>Yes — cost stops scaling with headcount</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>Cleanup and negotiation matter, but they don't change the underlying growth curve. Only the structural lever does that.</p>

<h2>3. Find where per-seat pricing is doing the most damage</h2>
<p>The clearest signal: which systems have a large population of light or occasional users — partners, field staff, customers, seasonal workers — paying full per-seat rates for a fraction of the platform's functionality? That mismatch, multiplied across headcount, is usually where the biggest structural opportunity lives, and it's the exact problem covered in <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/reduce-crm-licensing-costs">how to cut CRM licensing costs</a>.</p>

<h2>4. Don't propose a cut that breaks a workflow</h2>
<p>The fastest way to lose credibility on cost-cutting is proposing something that forces a team to give up real functionality. The structural fix — moving light users to a <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-portal-development">custom portal</a> synced live to the existing system — doesn't touch what power users do; it only changes how light users get access. That's the difference between a cut that sticks and one that gets quietly reversed six months later.</p>

<h2>5. Build the business case with real numbers, not estimates</h2>
<p>Run actual seat counts and growth trends through the <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/savings-calculator">savings calculator</a> before presenting a recommendation. A specific, defensible number moves a budget conversation further than a general sense that "software costs feel high."</p>

<h2>6. Revisit annually, not just at renewal</h2>
<p>Seat sprawl and shadow tooling accumulate continuously, not just at contract renewal — see <a href="https://cms.ardncloudsolutions.com/saas-seat-sprawl-mid-market/">SaaS seat sprawl at mid-market companies</a> for how this typically happens. Put a recurring cross-departmental review on the calendar rather than waiting for the next renewal cycle to notice.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Where should a CFO start if this hasn't been reviewed before?</h3>
<p>Start with the consolidated list from step 1 — most of the value in a first pass comes just from seeing every contract, seat count, and renewal date in one place for the first time.</p>

<h3>Is the structural fix (moving to flat-fee tools) always worth the disruption?</h3>
<p>It's worth evaluating whenever a light-user population is large or growing faster than your core team — the math either supports it clearly or it doesn't. The savings calculator is a faster way to find out than debating it internally.</p>

<h3>How do we get department buy-in instead of resistance?</h3>
<p>Lead with the fact that power users and core workflows aren't affected — the structural fix specifically targets people who are overpaying for underused access, not the tools your teams rely on daily.</p>
