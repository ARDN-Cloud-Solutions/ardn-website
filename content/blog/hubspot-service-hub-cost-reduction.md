<p>Service Hub seats are priced for full-time support agents running tickets, automation, and reporting all day. In practice, a lot of the people who touch tickets aren't full-time agents at all — they're occasional escalation contacts, team leads who check in once a day, or staff from adjacent teams who need to see a ticket but never work a queue. Paying full Service Hub rates for that group is the most common source of avoidable Service Hub spend.</p>

<h2>Who's actually a full Service Hub agent?</h2>
<p>Break your current seat list into two real groups, not by title but by behavior:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Full agents:</strong> work a ticket queue daily, use automation and SLAs, need full reporting access.</li>
  <li><strong>Light touch:</strong> occasionally check a ticket's status, add a note, or get looped in on an escalation — but don't run the queue.</li>
</ul>
<p>The second group is where the savings are. They're on a paid Service Hub seat today largely because it was the easiest way to give them visibility, not because they need the full toolset.</p>

<h2>Three ways to right-size</h2>

<h3>1. Move light-touch users off paid seats</h3>
<p>If their entire Service Hub usage fits on a short list — check status, add a note — they likely don't need Service Hub's paid tier at all. Free CRM access, or a purpose-built view, may cover it.</p>

<h3>2. Consolidate duplicate tooling</h3>
<p>It's common to find a separate ticketing or status tool doing a job Service Hub already covers (or vice versa) — reconcile before renewal instead of paying for both.</p>

<h3>3. Move status-checking entirely off Service Hub with a portal</h3>
<p>For internal teams, field staff, or customers who just need to see ticket status or add an update — not manage a queue — a <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-portal-development">custom portal</a> synced live to HubSpot's API through Service Hub's ticket data gives them exactly that, without a Service Hub seat at all. One flat monthly fee, regardless of how many light-touch users you add.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Will moving light-touch users off Service Hub slow down support?</h3>
<p>Not if it's scoped correctly. The goal is to move people who aren't running the queue anyway — full agents keep their seats and workflow exactly as-is.</p>

<h3>Can customers still see ticket status without a HubSpot login?</h3>
<p>Yes — a portal reads ticket data from HubSpot live through the API and shows customers exactly the status and updates you choose to expose, without giving them any access to HubSpot itself.</p>

<h3>Does this work if we use Service Hub alongside another support tool?</h3>
<p>Yes. A portal can be built to pull from whichever system holds the ticket data — Service Hub, another helpdesk, or both — so consolidating visibility doesn't require consolidating tools first.</p>
