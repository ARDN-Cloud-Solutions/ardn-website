<p>Learning management systems commonly bill per active learner per month — a model that only charges for people who actually log in and train during a billing cycle, which sounds efficient until a training program succeeds at reaching more people, and the bill rises in direct proportion to that success.</p>

<h2>Why per-active-learner pricing bites differently than per-seat</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Rewards low engagement, not high.</strong> A program with a small, disengaged learner base can look cheap on this pricing model, while a genuinely successful rollout that gets more people actively training costs proportionally more — the opposite of the incentive a training team actually wants.</li>
  <li><strong>Seasonal and cohort-based training spikes.</strong> Onboarding cohorts, compliance-refresh windows, and seasonal certification pushes all spike active-learner counts for a defined period, and the bill spikes with them even though the need is temporary.</li>
  <li><strong>External learners add up fast.</strong> Training extended to customers, partners, or channel resellers — not just employees — often counts against the same active-learner total, so external-facing training programs can drive cost as much as internal ones.</li>
  <li><strong>Content and course-authoring tiers stack on top.</strong> Beyond the learner count, advanced authoring tools, certifications, and reporting are frequently gated to higher pricing tiers, adding a second axis to the bill.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Where native per-learner pricing still fits</h2>
<p>For a stable, predictable training population — a fixed employee base training on a regular cadence — per-active-learner pricing is a reasonable, transparent model. It's the training programs designed to scale reach (compliance rollouts, customer education, partner enablement) where the pricing model works against the program's own goal.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does "active learner" mean anyone with an account, or only people who log in?</h3>
<p>Typically only those who log in and engage during the billing period — but confirm the exact definition with your vendor, since it directly determines what drives your bill.</p>

<h3>Can we negotiate a flat rate instead of per-active-learner billing?</h3>
<p>Some vendors offer enterprise or flat-fee tiers at higher volume commitments — worth asking about directly if learner count is trending upward and unpredictable.</p>

<h3>Is a custom training portal worth it for a small, stable employee base?</h3>
<p>Usually not — the case gets stronger as learner count grows, includes external audiences, or spikes seasonally in a way that makes per-learner billing unpredictable.</p>
