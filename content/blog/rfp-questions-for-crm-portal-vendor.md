<p>Most of the cost and lock-in problems companies discover after buying a CRM portal add-on were answerable before signing — they just weren't asked in the RFP. These are the questions that surface pricing structure, integration depth, and exit terms while there's still leverage to negotiate or walk away.</p>

<h2>Pricing and scaling questions</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Is pricing per seat, per active user, per login event, or tiered by total user count?</li>
  <li>What happens to the price if our user count doubles in year two — is there a rate lock, or does it reprice at renewal?</li>
  <li>Are there separate fees for admin seats, read-only seats, and full-access seats?</li>
  <li>What features are gated behind a higher tier that we'd need to reach for the use case we're buying this for?</li>
</ul>

<h2>Integration and data questions</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Does the portal read and write to our CRM live via API, or on a sync delay — and how long is that delay?</li>
  <li>Who owns the data generated inside the portal itself (activity logs, uploaded files, custom fields)?</li>
  <li>Can we export all portal-generated data in a usable format at any time, not just at contract end?</li>
</ul>

<h2>Contract and exit questions</h2>
<ul>
  <li>What's the notice period and does the contract auto-renew by default?</li>
  <li>Is there a minimum seat commitment, and can it be reduced mid-term if usage drops?</li>
  <li>What does migrating off this tool actually require — is there a documented export/exit process?</li>
</ul>

<h2>The question most RFPs skip</h2>
<p>Ask directly: "If our user count for this portal doubles, does our price double?" A vendor's answer tells you immediately whether you're buying a system that gets more expensive as adoption succeeds — which, for anything client- or member-facing, is the wrong incentive built into the pricing itself. The alternative worth comparing against is a <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-portal-development">custom-built portal</a> on a flat monthly fee, where that question has a fixed, unconditional answer.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Should we send these questions to every vendor, or just finalists?</h3>
<p>Send the pricing and scaling questions to every vendor in the initial RFP — they're cheap to ask and often eliminate poor fits early. Save the deeper integration and exit questions for the finalist round.</p>

<h3>What if a vendor won't answer the scaling question directly?</h3>
<p>Treat a vague or deflected answer as the answer — it usually means the pricing does scale unfavorably and the vendor would rather not say so before you're locked in.</p>

<h3>Is a custom build always cheaper than a portal add-on?</h3>
<p>Not always at small scale, but the crossover point is often lower than expected once seat growth is projected forward. Run the specific numbers rather than assuming either direction.</p>
