<p>Professional services automation (PSA) tools — the category covering time tracking, resource scheduling, project billing, and utilization reporting — are internal, staff-facing software, priced per user like most enterprise SaaS. That's a different cost problem from client-facing portals: this is about what it costs to run your own delivery team, not what it costs to give clients visibility.</p>

<h2>What drives PSA license cost</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Every biller needs a seat.</strong> Time tracking and utilization reporting require input from every billable employee, not just managers — so seat count tracks headcount closely, with little room to "only license the power users."</li>
  <li><strong>Tiered feature gating.</strong> Resource forecasting, advanced billing rules, and custom reporting are often locked behind a higher tier, pushing firms to over-license just to reach the features they actually need.</li>
  <li><strong>Add-on modules.</strong> Expense management, contract management, and BI/reporting are frequently separate add-ons with their own per-user pricing layered on top of the base PSA seat.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why this is harder to trim than a typical CRM audit</h2>
<p>The usual first move on license cost — reclaim dormant seats — barely applies here, because nearly every billable employee genuinely needs to log time and see their utilization. There isn't a large pool of "light users" to move off a seat the way there often is in a CRM. The bigger lever is whether the whole system needs to be a licensed, multi-tenant SaaS product at all, versus a system built specifically for the firm's own billing and resourcing rules.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Is it realistic to replace a PSA tool entirely, or just parts of it?</h3>
<p>Either works — some firms replace time entry and utilization reporting first while keeping the PSA tool for billing during a transition, others do a full cutover. Scope depends on how tightly the firm's billing rules are already tied to the incumbent tool's data.</p>

<h3>What about integrations with accounting software?</h3>
<p>A custom build integrates with whatever accounting or ERP system the firm already runs through its API — that's a scoping decision made at build time, not a limitation of moving off a licensed PSA tool.</p>

<h3>Does every professional services firm need a PSA tool at all?</h3>
<p>Most benefit from some system tracking time, utilization, and billing — the question this post addresses is whether that system has to be a per-seat SaaS product, not whether the function itself is needed.</p>
