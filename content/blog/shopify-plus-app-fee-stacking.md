<p>Shopify Plus's own platform fee is only the base layer. Most mid-market e-commerce brands running on Plus also run a stack of paid apps — subscriptions, reviews, loyalty, personalization, back-in-stock alerts, upsell/cross-sell — each with its own monthly fee, and many billed on a percentage of revenue or order count rather than a flat rate. That stack is what turns a reasonable base platform cost into a much larger total.</p>

<h2>Where the app-fee stack actually adds up</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Revenue-share and per-order app pricing.</strong> Subscription-management and loyalty apps in particular are frequently priced as a percentage of processed revenue or per-order fee, so app cost scales with sales growth — the opposite of what a growing brand wants from its cost structure.</li>
  <li><strong>Overlapping functionality across apps.</strong> Reviews, personalization, and upsell apps often duplicate parts of each other's functionality (on-site pop-ups, email triggers, analytics), meaning a brand can be paying twice for adjacent features.</li>
  <li><strong>Tier upgrades as store traffic grows.</strong> Many apps price by store traffic, order volume, or SKU count tiers — growth in any of those dimensions can force an app-level tier upgrade independent of the Shopify Plus platform fee itself.</li>
  <li><strong>App-to-app integration fees.</strong> Connecting apps to each other, or to an ERP/inventory system, sometimes requires a middleware app with its own subscription, adding a further layer on top of the core stack.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why this is easy to miss until a margin review</h2>
<p>Each app in the stack gets added one at a time, usually to solve a specific problem in the moment (a reviews widget, a subscription launch), and each one looks individually cheap against total revenue. It's only when someone totals the full monthly app spend against the Shopify Plus platform fee — and notices several apps are billed as a percentage of a revenue base that's now much larger than when the app was first installed — that the stack's true cost becomes visible.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Should a brand rip out its whole app stack at once?</h3>
<p>No — this is usually a gradual, app-by-app evaluation as each one's dollar cost grows large enough to justify a custom replacement, not an all-at-once migration.</p>

<h3>Does replacing an app with custom code still work inside Shopify Plus?</h3>
<p>Yes — a custom-built feature typically integrates via Shopify's APIs and stays inside the existing storefront, so checkout, inventory, and the core platform relationship stay intact.</p>

<h3>Which apps are the best first candidates to evaluate?</h3>
<p>Start with whichever apps bill as a percentage of revenue or per order rather than a flat fee — those are the ones whose dollar cost is growing fastest as sales scale, and the ones where a flat-fee custom build changes the cost trajectory the most.</p>
