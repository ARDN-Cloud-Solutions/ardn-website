<p>Landscaping and lawn care field-service platforms — Aspire, Service Autopilot, and LawnPro span the range from enterprise to small-shop — don't all price the same way, but the two higher-end platforms share a mechanic that per-technician dispatch software in other trades doesn't have quite as sharply: pricing tied to crew count, not a static year-round headcount. For a business whose staffing doubles every spring, that's a different cost problem than a plumbing dispatcher adding one technician mid-year.</p>

<h2>How the per-crew model actually works</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Entry pricing by crew, not by seat.</strong> Aspire's starter tier is reported at $39/month per crew, which sounds inexpensive until a business scales up seasonal crews to handle spring demand — each additional crew is a new line item, not a marginal add to a shared platform fee.</li>
  <li><strong>Full-platform pricing shifts to per-user.</strong> Move up to Aspire's full operations platform and the reported pricing shifts to roughly $300-500 per user per month, positioning it for established commercial landscaping operations rather than smaller shops.</li>
  <li><strong>Flat-tier ceilings that still gate features.</strong> Service Autopilot's higher tiers reportedly run up to around $499/month, with full pricing largely gated behind a sales call rather than published outright.</li>
  <li><strong>Seasonal crew scaling hits at the worst time.</strong> Spring ramp-up — hiring temporary crews to handle the demand spike — is also when material, fuel, and labor costs are climbing, so a per-crew software bill that multiplies in step with temp hiring compounds cost pressure exactly when margins are thinnest.</li>
</ul>

<p>This is a different mechanic from the per-technician, per-dispatcher pricing covered in <a href="https://cms.ardncloudsolutions.com/field-service-dispatch-software-seat-cost/">field service dispatch software seat costs</a> for HVAC, plumbing, and electrical trades — those trades run a comparatively stable year-round workforce, while landscaping's crew count swings seasonally by design, and a per-crew or per-user license that scales with that swing is a structurally different cost driver than a static per-technician count.</p>

<h2>Where the licensed platform still earns its keep</h2>
<p>Route optimization, job costing against material and labor, and crew GPS tracking carry real operational value that a purpose-built landscaping platform has spent years refining. That operational core — the part that actually plans the day's routes and tracks job profitability — is usually not where the savings opportunity lives.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a custom portal replace our route optimization and job-costing software?</h3>
<p>No — it's built to sync with your existing landscaping platform via API. Route planning, job costing, and crew GPS tracking stay in the operational system; the portal covers client communication and administrative work that doesn't need that routing data model.</p>

<h3>Why does our software bill spike every spring?</h3>
<p>If your platform prices per crew or per user, adding temporary crews for the spring demand surge adds licensed seats directly to the bill, on top of the seasonal labor and material cost increases the business is already absorbing.</p>

<h3>Is this the same cost problem as HVAC or plumbing dispatch software?</h3>
<p>Not quite — those trades typically license per technician or per dispatcher against a comparatively stable year-round headcount. Landscaping's per-crew pricing tracks a workforce that swings seasonally by design, which is a different scaling problem even when the underlying per-seat mechanic looks similar.</p>
