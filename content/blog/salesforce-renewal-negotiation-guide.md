<p>Salesforce renewal conversations tend to go one of two ways: you accept whatever the account executive proposes, or you show up with your own numbers and a credible alternative. The second version gets meaningfully better outcomes. Here's how to prepare for it.</p>

<h2>Start 90 days out, not 90 minutes out</h2>
<p>Negotiating leverage comes from having time to act on what you find — if you're auditing seats the week before renewal, you have no room to actually deactivate or migrate anyone before the deadline. Give yourself at least a full quarter.</p>

<h2>1. Run the audit first</h2>
<p>You can't negotiate from a position of strength without your own usage data. Pull last-login dates, license-type assignments, and feature usage where available. Our <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/license-guard">License Guard</a> app automates the dormant-seat scan on Salesforce if you don't want to build the report by hand; the full audit process is in our <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/blog/salesforce-license-audit-checklist">Salesforce license audit checklist</a>.</p>

<h2>2. Know your real number, not the list price</h2>
<p>List price is a starting point for the vendor, not the number you should expect to pay. Multi-year deals, competitive pressure, and your own usage trend (flat or shrinking active-user count vs. growing) all move the number. Come in knowing exactly how many seats you actually need — not how many you're currently paying for.</p>

<h2>3. Bring a credible alternative, not a bluff</h2>
<p>"Can we get a discount?" is a weak opening. "We're moving 120 light users off per-seat licenses onto a flat-fee portal unless the per-seat price comes down" is a different conversation entirely, because it's backed by a real plan you can execute either way. This only works if the alternative is genuine — plug your numbers into the <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/savings-calculator">savings calculator</a> so the alternative has real math behind it, not just a talking point.</p>

<h2>4. Negotiate term length deliberately</h2>
<p>Longer terms often come with better per-seat pricing, but they lock in your current seat count and tier mix for years. If you're mid-transition — reclaiming dormant seats, right-sizing tiers, or moving light users to a portal — a shorter term or a seat-count true-down clause protects you from paying for capacity you're actively shedding.</p>

<h2>5. Push for true-down rights, not just true-up</h2>
<p>Most contracts let Salesforce add seats mid-term (true-up) but don't let you remove them until renewal (true-down). If you're actively reducing seat count, ask explicitly for the ability to true down at agreed checkpoints — it's a reasonable ask, especially on multi-year terms.</p>

<h2>6. Separate "what we use" from "what we're locked into"</h2>
<p>Bundles and add-ons accumulate over time. Before renewal, list every SKU on the contract and confirm someone actually uses it. Unused add-ons are pure margin for the vendor and pure waste for you — cut them before you negotiate the core seats.</p>

<h2>What negotiation can't fix</h2>
<p>Negotiation moves the per-seat rate. It doesn't change the fact that per-seat pricing scales with headcount forever — a great renewal this year doesn't stop the bill from climbing again in three years as you hire. For the structural fix, see the full framework in <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/reduce-crm-licensing-costs">how to cut CRM licensing costs</a>, which covers moving light users to a flat-fee portal so the number stops growing with your team.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>How much of a discount is realistic?</h3>
<p>It varies by org size, competitive situation, and timing in Salesforce's fiscal quarter — there's no universal number, and anyone quoting one specific percentage is guessing. What's consistent is that documented usage data and a real alternative both improve your position regardless of the final figure.</p>

<h3>Does Salesforce negotiate harder at certain times of year?</h3>
<p>Sales teams generally have more flexibility near the end of their fiscal quarter or year, when they're motivated to close. It's not guaranteed, but timing your renewal conversation to align with that window doesn't hurt.</p>

<h3>Should we mention we're evaluating alternatives?</h3>
<p>Only if it's true. A specific, funded plan — like moving a defined group of light users to a portal — is legitimate leverage. A vague threat with no follow-through plan is easy for an experienced AE to see through and can undermine your credibility in future renewals.</p>
