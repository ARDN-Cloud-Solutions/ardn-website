<p>Practice management platforms built for law firms typically offer a client portal as an add-on module, and that module is commonly priced per active client login — the same per-seat logic used for internal staff seats, applied to a group (clients) the firm doesn't control the size or activity pattern of. A client checking in once during a slow month and a client checking in daily during active litigation can land in different pricing tiers for reasons that have nothing to do with the firm's actual cost to serve them.</p>

<h2>What clients actually want from a portal</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Matter status</strong> — where things stand, without a call to the paralegal.</li>
  <li><strong>Document exchange</strong> — secure upload and download, avoiding email attachments for anything sensitive.</li>
  <li><strong>Invoice and trust account visibility</strong> — billing status and retainer balance on demand.</li>
  <li><strong>Secure messaging</strong> — a channel that stays inside privileged, access-controlled infrastructure rather than a personal email thread.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why confidentiality makes this different from a typical client portal</h2>
<p>A law firm's client portal carries privilege and confidentiality obligations that a marketing agency's client dashboard doesn't. That doesn't change the pricing-model problem — per-seat cost still scales the wrong direction as the client roster grows — but it does mean access controls, audit logging, and document handling need to be built to the standard the firm already applies to client files, not a generic vendor default.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a custom portal replace our practice management software?</h3>
<p>No — it's a client-facing layer synced to your existing practice management system through its API. The firm keeps using its practice management platform exactly as before.</p>

<h3>Can it support multiple matters per client with different access levels?</h3>
<p>Yes, when scoped at build time — per-matter access, delegated access for a client's own staff, and role-based visibility are all design decisions made up front rather than limitations of the flat-fee model.</p>

<h3>What about e-signature for engagement letters and documents?</h3>
<p>A custom build can integrate with your firm's existing e-signature provider, or the workflow can be scoped during the build if you don't have one in place yet.</p>
