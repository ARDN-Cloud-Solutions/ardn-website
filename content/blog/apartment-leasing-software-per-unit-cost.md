<p><a href="https://cms.ardncloudsolutions.com/property-management-software-per-unit-cost/">Property management software's per-unit pricing</a> covers the owner-facing side of the platform — accounting, maintenance dispatch, owner reporting. The leasing and resident-facing layer on top of it — online applications, resident communication, maintenance requests, the resident portal itself — is billed separately in a lot of these platforms, and it has its own per-unit pricing quirks worth understanding on their own terms.</p>

<h2>How leasing/resident-portal pricing tends to work</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Per-unit-per-month is the dominant mechanic</strong>, whether the resident portal is bundled into the core property management plan or licensed as a distinct module.</li>
  <li><strong>Minimum monthly fees are common</strong>, and they change the effective per-unit rate meaningfully for smaller portfolios — a portfolio well under a platform's target size can end up paying several times the advertised per-unit rate once the minimum is applied.</li>
  <li><strong>Per-transaction fees layer on top</strong> — online rent payment processing frequently carries its own per-transaction charge, separate from the base subscription.</li>
  <li><strong>Fully custom quotes at the enterprise end</strong> — larger platforms serving big portfolios often don't publish per-unit rates at all for the resident-portal layer, quoting instead based on total portfolio size and module mix.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Where the minimum-fee math bites</h2>
<p>The advertised per-unit rate is the headline number, but the minimum monthly fee is what actually determines cost for a portfolio below the platform's target size. A landlord with a modest portfolio can end up paying an effective per-unit rate several times higher than the number in the pricing page, simply because the total unit count doesn't clear the minimum — a structural quirk that rewards scale and penalizes exactly the portfolios that most need to watch cost per unit.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Can a custom resident portal integrate with our existing accounting/PM system?</h3>
<p>Yes — a resident portal is typically scoped to read and write to whatever accounting or PM system stays in place, rather than replacing it, connecting through that system's API.</p>

<h3>Does dropping a bundled resident portal mean losing online rent payments?</h3>
<p>No — payment processing is scoped as part of the custom build, connecting to your chosen payment processor rather than requiring the bundled platform's built-in processor.</p>

<h3>Is this worth evaluating below the platform's minimum-fee threshold?</h3>
<p>That's exactly where the case is clearest — a portfolio paying a high effective per-unit rate because it's under a vendor's minimum is paying a penalty a flat monthly fee doesn't have.</p>
