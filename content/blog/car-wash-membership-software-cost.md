<p>Unlimited wash clubs have become the primary revenue model for car washes, and the software that runs them — RFID tag recognition, recurring billing, self-serve enrollment and plan management — is priced in a way that's genuinely different from most recurring-membership software. Rather than a flat per-location SaaS fee, some car wash membership platforms take a revenue share of the monthly membership dues collected through the system, meaning the vendor's cut grows automatically as the membership base grows.</p>

<h2>How this pricing mechanic differs from a flat SaaS fee</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Revenue share on membership dues, not a flat license.</strong> Some vendors in this category charge a percentage of the recurring dues processed through the platform rather than (or in addition to) a fixed monthly software fee — the cost line grows in direct proportion to membership growth.</li>
  <li><strong>Hardware and RFID tag costs sit outside the software fee.</strong> Tag readers, in-lane hardware, and physical membership tags are typically billed separately from whatever software or revenue-share arrangement covers the billing and enrollment platform.</li>
  <li><strong>Per-location fees still apply on top.</strong> Multi-site operators frequently pay a per-location base charge in addition to whatever revenue share or per-transaction fee applies to membership billing.</li>
  <li><strong>Add-on modules for marketing and loyalty.</strong> Automated win-back campaigns, tiered membership plans, and customer-facing mobile apps are commonly priced as separate add-ons layered onto the base platform.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why revenue-share pricing is a different problem than per-seat pricing</h2>
<p>A per-seat or per-location fee is at least a fixed, budgetable number. A revenue-share arrangement on membership dues means the exact thing that makes an unlimited wash club a good business — a growing, loyal recurring-revenue base — is also what makes the software increasingly expensive, with no ceiling tied to how much system capacity or support the operator is actually using.</p>

<h2>Where the fix actually lives</h2>
<p>RFID hardware and lane equipment stay exactly as they are — this isn't a hardware replacement question. What can move is the billing, enrollment, and membership-management software layer sitting on top of that hardware: a <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-portal-development">custom-built membership and billing portal</a> can run recurring dues collection and self-serve plan management on a flat monthly fee instead of a percentage of dues that climbs with every new member signed up. This is the same structural argument as <a href="https://cms.ardncloudsolutions.com/gym-membership-software-per-location-cost/">gym membership software's per-location costs</a>, applied to a revenue-share mechanic instead of a per-location-plus-member-tier one — see <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/reduce-crm-licensing-costs">how to reduce recurring software costs</a> for the general pattern.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does replacing the membership software mean replacing RFID hardware or in-lane equipment?</h3>
<p>No — a custom membership and billing layer is scoped to integrate with existing hardware and lane equipment rather than requiring new physical infrastructure, unless a hardware refresh is separately part of the project.</p>

<h3>Is revenue-share pricing always disclosed clearly upfront?</h3>
<p>Not always as a single obvious line item — some operators report the revenue-share component being less visible than a flat subscription fee would be, which is exactly why it's worth asking a current or prospective vendor for the full fee structure, including any percentage-of-dues component, before signing.</p>

<h3>Does a flat-fee custom build handle tiered membership plans and win-back campaigns?</h3>
<p>Yes — plan tiers, promotional pricing, and automated retention campaigns are scoped at build time to the operator's actual membership structure, rather than sold as separate add-on modules with their own fees.</p>
