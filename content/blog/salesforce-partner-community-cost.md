<p>A Salesforce partner community (built on Experience Cloud) is a common way to give resellers, brokers, dealers, or franchisees access to deal registration, inventory, or account data without handing them a full internal seat. It's the right instinct — external users shouldn't get internal licenses — but the license model underneath still bills per login, and partner networks have a habit of growing faster than anyone budgeted for.</p>

<h2>How partner community licensing actually works</h2>
<p>Experience Cloud partner licenses are sold in login-based or member-based models, priced well below an internal Sales/Service Cloud seat but still per-user. The math that catches teams off guard: a partner network isn't static headcount like an internal team. Add ten new dealers or brokers this quarter and the license bill moves immediately, with no equivalent revenue-per-partner assumption built into the budget.</p>

<h2>Where the cost creeps in</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Partner turnover.</strong> Reseller and broker relationships churn — new partners onboard, old ones go dormant, and licenses often don't get cleaned up in between.</li>
  <li><strong>Seasonal or low-activity partners.</strong> A dealer who logs in twice a quarter to check an order status is billed the same as one who lives in the portal daily.</li>
  <li><strong>Growth by design.</strong> If partner growth is the actual growth strategy — more resellers, more franchisees — the per-seat model taxes your own success.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The structural alternative: a portal, not a community license</h2>
<p>What most partners actually need is narrow: check deal status, register a lead, pull inventory, download a spec sheet. That's a smaller surface area than the full Experience Cloud community license assumes. A <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-partner-portal-development">custom partner portal</a> built to that exact scope, synced live to Salesforce as the system of record, replaces the per-login fee with one flat monthly fee — whether you have 20 partners or 200.</p>

<h2>When Experience Cloud still makes sense</h2>
<p>If partners genuinely need broad, evolving access — building their own reports, using multiple Salesforce-native features, or the network is small and stable — the native community can be the simpler path. The flat-fee case gets stronger specifically when partner count is growing, when partner needs are narrow and repetitive, or both.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a custom partner portal replace Salesforce?</h3>
<p>No. Salesforce stays the system of record; the portal is a purpose-built front end for your partner network that reads and writes to Salesforce through its API.</p>

<h3>Can we migrate an existing Experience Cloud community to a flat-fee portal?</h3>
<p>Yes — existing partner workflows and data mapping carry over; the migration replaces the licensed front end, not your underlying Salesforce data or processes.</p>

<h3>Is this only relevant for large partner networks?</h3>
<p>The savings scale with partner count, but the model matters most whenever partner growth is a business goal — the per-seat cost otherwise grows in lockstep with something you're actively trying to expand.</p>
