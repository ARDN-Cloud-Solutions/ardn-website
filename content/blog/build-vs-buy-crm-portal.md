<p>Once you've decided that light CRM users — field staff, partners, customers — shouldn't be on a full per-seat license, the next question is how to give them access instead. There are two real paths: buy a pre-built portal product that plugs into your CRM, or have one built to your exact workflow. Both solve the seat-cost problem; they differ on fit, control, and what happens as you scale.</p>

<h2>Buying a pre-built portal product</h2>
<p>Several vendors sell portal add-ons or standalone products designed to sit on top of a CRM and expose a subset of data to external users. The appeal is speed — a pre-built product can be configured and live faster than a from-scratch build, and someone else maintains the underlying code.</p>

<h3>Where "buy" strains</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Pricing often reintroduces the per-seat problem</strong> — many portal products still bill per active external user or per login, just at a lower rate than a full CRM seat.</li>
  <li><strong>You're working within someone else's component set</strong> — customization is bounded by what the product's configuration options allow.</li>
  <li><strong>A second vendor relationship</strong> — another contract, another renewal, another point of integration risk between your CRM and the portal product itself.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Building a custom portal</h2>
<p>A <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/custom-portal-development">custom-built portal</a>, synced live to your CRM through its API, is built around your exact workflow rather than a general-purpose template — and priced as one flat monthly fee regardless of how many external users touch it.</p>

<h3>Where "build" strains</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Longer to first version</strong> than configuring an off-the-shelf product — though with the right partner this is typically weeks, not months.</li>
  <li><strong>Depends on the build partner</strong> — quality and ongoing support vary a lot depending on who builds and runs it.</li>
</ul>

<h2>How to actually decide</h2>
<p>If your external-user workflow is genuinely standard — a common use case the pre-built product was designed for, with a small and stable user count — buying can be the faster, lower-effort path. If the workflow is specific to how your business actually operates, the user count is growing, or per-user pricing on the "bought" option would scale into a real cost over time, building starts to win on both cost and fit. Run your own numbers through the <a href="https://ardncloudsolutions.com/savings-calculator">savings calculator</a> before committing either way — it's a clearer test than intuition.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Is buying always cheaper upfront?</h3>
<p>Often, yes — configuration is typically faster and cheaper than a from-scratch build in the first month. The comparison changes once you look at total cost over a few years, especially if the bought product's pricing scales with user count.</p>

<h3>Can we start by buying and switch to building later?</h3>
<p>Yes, and it's a reasonable path if you're not yet sure how the external-user population will grow. The CRM data underneath doesn't change; only the front end and pricing model do when you eventually rebuild.</p>

<h3>Does a custom-built portal require replacing our CRM?</h3>
<p>No. It's built to sync with your existing CRM through its API — your CRM stays the system of record either way, whether you buy or build the portal layer on top of it.</p>
