<p>Once you've decided your light CRM users (partners, clients, occasional internal staff) don't need full seats, the next decision is how to build the alternative. Broadly there are three paths: a no-code/low-code portal builder, an off-the-shelf vertical SaaS portal product, or a custom-built portal. Each has a real, different cost and risk profile.</p>

<h2>Option 1: No-code/low-code portal tools</h2>
<p>Fast to stand up, and often billed per user or per active viewer — which can reintroduce the exact per-seat problem you were trying to escape, just under a different vendor. They're also constrained to what the builder supports; workflows outside its templates get awkward fast. Good for a simple, static need; a weaker fit once the workflow gets specific.</p>

<h2>Option 2: Off-the-shelf vertical SaaS portal products</h2>
<p>Purpose-built for a given industry (insurance, hospitality, membership), these can fit well out of the box if your process matches the vendor's assumptions closely. The tradeoff is the same one you're already trying to avoid: another per-seat or per-tier subscription, plus limited ability to shape it to your exact workflow when it doesn't quite fit.</p>

<h2>Option 3: Custom-built portal</h2>
<p>Built to your exact workflow, integrated directly with your CRM's API, and — when built on a flat monthly fee model like ARDN's — priced independently of how many users touch it. The tradeoff is upfront build time and choosing the right partner; it's not instant like a no-code template.</p>

<h2>How to actually decide</h2>
<p>The decision comes down to two questions: how standard is the workflow, and how much does usage grow over time? A simple, static need with few users can reasonably start with a no-code tool. A workflow specific to your business, or a user base (clients, partners, seasonal staff) that grows independently of your core team, is where a flat-fee custom build pays for itself — because the alternative's cost line keeps moving with something you don't control.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Is a custom build always the cheaper option?</h3>
<p>Not always — for a very small, stable, standard use case, an off-the-shelf tool can be cheaper in the short term. The custom build wins as user count, growth, or workflow specificity increases.</p>

<h3>How long does a custom portal actually take to build?</h3>
<p>ARDN builds custom portals in weeks, not months — the flat-fee model is designed around fast delivery, not a multi-quarter project.</p>

<h3>Can we start with a no-code tool and move to custom later?</h3>
<p>Yes, and it's a common path — validate the workflow cheaply, then move to a flat-fee custom build once the user base or workflow complexity outgrows the no-code tool's limits.</p>
