<p>Hotel and hospitality operations software — property management systems, booking platforms, staff scheduling tools — is typically priced per user or per property, following the same model as any other business SaaS. Hospitality has a structural feature that makes this model expensive in practice: a large share of the workforce is part-time, seasonal, or high-turnover, and most of them only need a narrow slice of what a full seat provides.</p>

<h2>Why hospitality staffing makes per-seat pricing worse</h2>

<h3>High seasonal variation</h3>
<p>Staffing swings significantly between peak and off-season. Per-seat software either bills for capacity you don't need in the slow months, or forces constant re-provisioning as headcount flexes — both are administrative and financial friction that a flat-fee model doesn't have.</p>

<h3>High turnover in front-line roles</h3>
<p>Front desk, housekeeping, and food & beverage roles typically turn over faster than corporate roles. Every departure and every new hire is a licensing event on a per-seat system — deactivate one, provision another, repeat continuously.</p>

<h3>Narrow, task-specific usage</h3>
<p>A housekeeper checking room status, a front-desk agent updating a reservation note, a shift lead approving time-off — none of these need the full depth of a property management system. They need one screen, done well, not a general-purpose platform license.</p>
