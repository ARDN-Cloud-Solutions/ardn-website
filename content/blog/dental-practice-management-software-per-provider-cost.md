<p>A single-location dental practice and a multi-location Dental Service Organization (DSO) face the same category of software — Dentrix Ascend, Curve Dental, Denticon, and similar platforms — but very different cost curves. A solo practice mostly adds cost by adding providers. A DSO adds cost by adding locations, and that's a different, often steeper scaling problem than the per-provider math a single office runs.</p>

<h2>How dental PM pricing actually splits</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Per-provider subscription models</strong> — common for single- and few-location practices, billing scales with licensed dentist/hygienist count.</li>
  <li><strong>Per-location base fees</strong> — cloud platforms aimed at multi-site groups more often price a base fee per office, sometimes with provider count layered on top as a hybrid model rather than a pure per-seat charge.</li>
  <li><strong>Legacy on-premise licensing</strong> — older systems like Eaglesoft were traditionally sold as a larger upfront license plus per-workstation cost, a different cost shape entirely from the newer cloud subscription models.</li>
  <li><strong>Clearinghouse and claims fees bundled in</strong> — dental billing runs on the 837D EDI claim format and dental-specific clearinghouse routing, distinct from the medical 837P stack. These transaction fees are frequently bundled into the subscription rather than itemized, which makes true per-claim cost hard to compare across vendors.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why DSO growth scales differently than a single practice adding providers</h2>
<p>A single practice hiring another hygienist adds one more per-provider line item — linear, predictable. A DSO opening its fifth location isn't just adding providers at that location; it's adding a new location-level base fee on top, and that fee recurs for every site in the portfolio. Vendors marketing specifically to DSOs (Denticon is positioned this way) typically move buyers off any published per-unit rate card entirely and into a custom enterprise agreement once the group crosses a handful of locations — which is itself a signal that off-the-shelf per-location pricing doesn't hold up well at DSO scale.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Does a custom build handle dental-specific claims formats?</h3>
<p>Yes — the 837D format and dental clearinghouse routing are integration requirements scoped at build time, connecting to the DSO's existing clearinghouse relationships rather than requiring new ones.</p>

<h3>Is this different from generic medical practice management software?</h3>
<p>Yes — dental billing uses its own EDI claim format and payer network, distinct from the medical 837P clearinghouse stack. A dental-specific system needs those connections built in, not adapted from a medical PM template.</p>

<h3>At what point does DSO scale make a custom build worth evaluating?</h3>
<p>Once per-location fees are recurring across enough sites that the group has already moved to a custom quote with its current vendor, the comparison is worth running — that's usually the same point at which the vendor itself has acknowledged the published rate card no longer fits.</p>
